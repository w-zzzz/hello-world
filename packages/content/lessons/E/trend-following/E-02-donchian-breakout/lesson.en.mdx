# Donchian channel breakout

A breakout strategy makes a simple bet: when price pushes past the highest level it has reached in a while, *something* has changed, and the move is more likely to continue than to immediately reverse. The Donchian channel turns that intuition into a precise, testable rule. You saw the channel as an indicator in Track B (B-19); here we build a tradable strategy around it.

The whole strategy fits on an index card — which is both its charm and its danger.

## The rule: enter on an N-bar high, exit on an M-bar low

A Donchian breakout system for a long-only trend trader is:

- **Entry:** buy when today's close (or high) exceeds the highest high of the prior `N` bars.
- **Exit:** sell when price falls below the lowest low of the prior `M` bars, with `M < N`.

A common pairing is a 20-bar entry channel and a 10-bar exit channel. Formally, the upper and lower channels are:

$$
U_t = \max_{1 \le i \le N} H_{t-i}, \qquad L_t = \min_{1 \le i \le M} L_{t-i}
$$

You go long when $\text{Close}_t > U_t$ and you flat the position when $\text{Close}_t < L_t$. There is no forecast, no oscillator, no opinion — only the question *are we at a new extreme?*

## The chase: you are always late, on purpose

By construction, a breakout entry buys *after* price has already risen to a new high. You will never buy the exact bottom. This feels uncomfortable, but it is the entire mechanism: you are paying a small, known cost (buying late) in exchange for only participating in moves that have already proven they have momentum. The strategy accepts many small losses and a few large wins; the math only works if you let the winners run via a slow exit channel.

## Whipsaws and the need for a filter

<Pitfall title="Range markets shred naked breakouts">
A pure Donchian breakout has no idea whether the market is trending or ranging. In a sideways, choppy market, price repeatedly pokes above the channel (you buy), then falls back through the exit (you sell at a loss), then pokes up again — a death by a thousand whipsaws. The fix is a regime filter: only take breakouts when a longer-term trend agrees (for example, price above its 200-day moving average) or when realized volatility is expanding. The breakout supplies the timing; the filter decides whether to listen.
</Pitfall>

## Tuning the channel lengths

The choice of `N` and `M` is a trade-off, not an optimization target to squeeze:

- **Longer `N`** (e.g. 55 bars): fewer, higher-quality signals; you give up early gains but suffer fewer false starts.
- **Shorter `N`** (e.g. 10 bars): more signals, faster response, but far more whipsaws.
- **`M` controls give-back:** a tight exit (small `M`) locks profits but exits trends prematurely; a loose exit rides trends but returns more on the reversal.

Resist the urge to grid-search the single best pair on past data — that is curve-fitting, and the result rarely survives out of sample (you will meet this formally in Track D).

<KeyPoint title="Core idea">
A Donchian breakout is a timing engine, not a complete edge. It mechanically buys new highs and sells new lows; its profitability lives or dies on a regime filter that keeps it out of range-bound chop.
</KeyPoint>
