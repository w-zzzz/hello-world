# Crypto funding-rate carry

Crypto exchanges invented an instrument that does not exist in traditional markets: the *perpetual swap* (or 'perp') — a futures contract with no expiry. To keep its price tethered to the underlying spot, exchanges use a periodic payment called the *funding rate*, exchanged directly between longs and shorts. That funding rate is a recurring cash flow, and harvesting it is one of the most popular carry trades in crypto.

The trade is a delta-neutral cash-and-carry: hold the asset, short the perp, and collect funding while being immune to price direction. Clean in theory. The danger, as always with carry, hides in the tail.

## What the funding rate is

A perpetual has no expiry to force convergence, so the exchange nudges its price toward spot with funding. When the perp trades *above* spot (longs are aggressive), longs pay shorts; when it trades below, shorts pay longs. The payment, made every 8 hours on most venues, is roughly:

$$
\text{funding payment} = \text{position notional} \times \text{funding rate}
$$

When sentiment is bullish, perps trade at a premium and funding is persistently positive — longs continuously pay shorts. That positive funding is the yield a carry trader wants to capture.

## Cash-and-carry: harvesting positive funding

To collect positive funding without betting on price, go market-neutral:

- **Long spot** — buy the actual coin (e.g. 1 BTC).
- **Short the perp** — sell 1 BTC of perpetual swap.

Your net price exposure is near zero: if BTC drops, the spot loses but the short perp gains by the same amount. Meanwhile, as the short side of a positively funded market, you *receive* the funding payment every 8 hours. The annualized carry is just the funding rate compounded:

$$
\text{APR} \approx \left(1 + r_f\right)^{3 \times 365} - 1
$$

where $r_f$ is the per-8-hour funding rate. A modest $0.01\%$ per period compounds to roughly $11\%$ a year — and crypto funding has at times run far higher.

## The basis

The same idea expressed in dated futures is the *basis*: the gap between the futures price $F$ and spot $S$. A positive basis (futures above spot) is the term-structure cousin of positive funding, and it converges to zero at expiry — a locked-in carry if you hold to settlement.

$$
\text{basis} = \frac{F - S}{S}
$$

Crypto carry behaves like an equity carry only in calm regimes. Compare a quiet stretch on SPY to a crypto de-pegging event: the carry yield looks like a smooth bond coupon right up until the moment it doesn't.

## The tail that wipes the carry

<Pitfall title="Funding flips, exchanges fail, liquidations cascade">
The carry can be erased by a single shock. Funding rates *flip sign*: in a sharp sell-off, perps trade below spot and the carry trader suddenly *pays* instead of receives. Exchange and counterparty risk is real — your collateral sits on a platform that can freeze, get hacked, or become insolvent (FTX). And a violent spike can trigger *liquidations*: if your short-perp margin is wiped before the spot leg can compensate, the supposedly delta-neutral trade blows up. A stablecoin de-peg or a funding spike can take back months of carry in a day. Size for the failure mode, not the steady coupon.
</Pitfall>

<KeyPoint title="Core idea">
Crypto funding carry harvests the perpetual-swap funding rate via a delta-neutral cash-and-carry: long spot, short perp, collect positive funding every 8 hours. The yield is real and can be large, but funding flips sign, exchanges fail, and liquidations can wipe out the carry in a single spike or de-peg.
</KeyPoint>
