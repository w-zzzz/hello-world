# Spot the divergence

Time to test your eye. The previous lesson taught you what divergence is and the four flavors it comes in. This lesson is a practice arena: five short scenarios, each describing a price-and-indicator setup, and you have to call it. Regular bullish, regular bearish, hidden bullish, hidden bearish, or no divergence at all.

A reminder of the cheat sheet you should be holding in your head:

| Type | Price | Indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Regular bullish | LL | HL |
| Regular bearish | HH | LH |
| Hidden bullish | HL | LL |
| Hidden bearish | LH | HH |

Anything that doesn't match one of these rows is "no divergence" — including the very common case where price and indicator simply agree (both higher highs, or both lower lows). That is **confirmation**, not divergence.

The trick to this game is to identify the **two swing points** first, then check their relation, then check the indicator's relation at those same two points. Don't try to glance and guess.

## What this practice doesn't teach you

You just got the right answers on a chart that has already happened. The hard part — the part this game cannot simulate — is committing to a swing structure **as it forms**, before you know how the next 5-20 bars will resolve. When you have to decide in real time, you also have to count every "almost-divergence" that turned out to be nothing — and most of them do.

<Pitfall title="The hindsight pattern recognition trap">
This game gave you scenarios where the two swings were already labeled. In real trading, you have to decide whether bar $t$ is a swing point, and then whether it forms a divergence with the previous swing point — all before bar $t+k$ has been printed. The divergence that looks obvious in hindsight wasn't obvious during its formation. Confirmation lag is part of the trade; if you can't accept the latency, you can't honestly trade divergences.
</Pitfall>

<KeyPoint title="Build the muscle, then check yourself">
Recognizing the four flavors quickly is necessary but not sufficient. After this game you know what a divergence looks like; before you can trade them, you also need a swing-confirmation algorithm and the discipline to log every divergence — including the ones that don't pay.
</KeyPoint>

## Where to go next

In real strategies, divergence is rarely a standalone signal. It is one input into a confluence stack (Lesson C-01) combined with a regime filter (next module: C-05). The math beneath looks like:

$$
\text{Score}_t = w_1 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{regime ok}] + w_2 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{divergence}] + w_3 \cdot \mathbb{1}[\text{confluence ok}]
$$

Enter when $\text{Score}_t$ exceeds a threshold. The divergence is one term; never the whole sum.
