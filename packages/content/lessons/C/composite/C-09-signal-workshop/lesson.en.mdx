# Building a composite signal end-to-end

This is the workshop lesson. We'll take everything from Track C — confluence, regime, aggregation, smoothing, filtering — and build one concrete composite signal on SPY, narrating the choices honestly so you can repeat the process on your own ideas. **This is not a strategy you should deploy.** It is a teaching scaffold for how to encode rules carefully.

## Step 1: state the hypothesis

> "On SPY, dip-buys during a healthy uptrend in a calm volatility environment tend to recover within a few weeks."

Write it down in plain language *first*. If you cannot, you don't have a hypothesis — you have a graph someone showed you. The hypothesis names three axes (trend, momentum, volatility) and one timeframe (a few weeks).

## Step 2: encode each axis as a rule

We map each axis to one rule, deliberately keeping the rules round-numbered and pre-chosen, not optimized:

- **Trend filter:** price > 200-day SMA. From C-08, this is the cheapest, most-defensible filter.
- **Momentum trigger:** RSI(14) crosses up through 40 from below. We use 40 instead of 50 because we want a "dip recovering" event, not a "trend continuing" one.
- **Regime filter:** realized volatility is in the bottom 80% of its trailing 252-day distribution. From C-05, this avoids the "calm dip" turning into a "crisis dip".

Aggregation style (from C-06): **AND**. All three must hold simultaneously.

$$
S_t \;=\; \mathbb{1}[\,\text{close}_t > \mathrm{SMA}_{200,t}\,] \;\cdot\; \mathbb{1}[\,\text{RSI}_{14,t-1} < 40 < \mathrm{RSI}_{14,t}\,] \;\cdot\; \mathbb{1}[\,\mathrm{RV}_t < q_{0.80}(\mathrm{RV})\,]
$$

## Step 3: define the exit, not just the entry

Beginners obsess over entries. Professionals know exits do most of the work. We give the trade three independent exits, whichever comes first:

1. **Profit target:** RSI(14) > 70 — momentum has carried us into overbought territory.
2. **Trend break:** close < 200-day SMA — the regime assumption no longer holds.
3. **Time stop:** 20 trading days elapsed — if it hasn't worked in a month, the thesis is stale.

The time stop matters more than people think. It bounds your maximum holding period, which bounds your variance and gives you cleaner statistics.

## Step 4: nail down execution detail

The single most common backtest lie is acting on the same bar's close. Be explicit:

- **Signal computed:** on the close of bar $t$, using only data available at bar $t$.
- **Order placed:** at the open of bar $t + 1$.
- **Fill assumption:** at the open price of bar $t + 1$, possibly with a slippage haircut.

Write this down in the code as a single function that takes bar $t$'s OHLCV and returns the entry/exit decision for bar $t + 1$. If you can't, you have lookahead somewhere.

## Step 5: check for lookahead

Hunt your code for any value computed from data unavailable at decision time:

- If the SMA uses *future* bars, you have lookahead.
- The 80th percentile of realized vol must use data ending at $t-1$, not $t$.
- RSI(14) at bar $t$ should use closes through bar $t$, no later.

The one-line check: replay bar-by-bar with each bar hidden after consumption — same trades?

## Step 6: test out-of-sample

Reserve at least the most recent 2 years and **do not look at it** while developing the rules. Run the finished signal once, end-to-end, on the held-out window. If it works similarly to in-sample, you have weak evidence of generalization. If it doesn't, accept that — do not "tweak" the rules on the now-leaked window.

<Pitfall title="This is a scaffold, not a strategy">
The signal you just walked through is not something to deploy. It is illustrative of the **process**: hypothesis → axis decomposition → AND aggregation → explicit exits → execution honesty → lookahead check → out-of-sample. Repeat the process on your own hypothesis. Resist the urge to backtest this exact rule, see a number you like, and call it done.
</Pitfall>

<KeyPoint title="The workshop in one sentence">
A composite signal is the disciplined encoding of a clear hypothesis, with exits and execution defined as carefully as the entry, validated honestly against data you held out before you started.
</KeyPoint>
