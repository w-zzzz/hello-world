# Track C wrap-up

Ten lessons in, you have seen confluence, divergence, regime, aggregation, smoothing, filtering, and a complete worked composite signal. This closing lesson is a deliberately short one — it consolidates the mental model you should be carrying out of Track C and signposts what comes next.

## Five things to remember

### 1. A signal is a rule, not a prediction

The signals you have built do not claim to know the future. They are **rules** that *behave* in known ways across history and (you hope) generalize. The right question is never "is this signal right?" — it is "in what regime is this signal *less wrong*?" Good signals fail gracefully: they lose small in the wrong regime and win large in the right one.

### 2. Confluence stacks across axes, not within one axis

Stacking RSI, Stochastic, and Williams %R does not give you three signals. It gives you one momentum signal with three sleeves. Real confluence stacks **independent axes**: trend, momentum, volatility, and flow. The math is in C-06:

$$
\mathrm{Var}\!\left(\bar S\right) \;=\; \frac{\sigma^2}{N}\,\bigl(1 + (N-1)\,\rho\bigr)
$$

When $\rho$ is large, $N$ doesn't help you. The whole game of confluence is finding low-$\rho$ ingredients.

### 3. Regime matters — every signal has a preferred environment

Every rule has a regime in which it is at its best and a regime in which it is at its worst. **Track which regime you are in**, and use it as a filter to suppress trades in obviously hostile environments. Don't try to predict the next regime — you cannot — but do refuse to run mean reversion through a runaway trend.

### 4. Filters trade frequency for hit-rate

You can layer trend, vol, time-of-day, and persistence filters to lift per-trade hit-rate, but each one is paid for in cut trades. Cross the threshold of three or four filters and you are no longer designing — you are curve-fitting. Each filter must have an independent justification.

### 5. Smoothing has a cost

Every bar of smoothing is a bar of lag. The optimal smoothing parameter depends on the regime, which is why robust strategies use round-number windows chosen a priori — not grid-searched.

## The signal-builder's toolkit

<Glossary>
**Confluence** — Multiple independent signals agreeing on a single direction. Power comes from axis independence, not from signal count.

**Regime** — A window of relatively stable market behaviour (trend vs range, high vs low vol, risk-on vs risk-off). Used as a filter, never as a forecast.

**Aggregation** — AND (high precision), OR (high recall), or weighted voting (the tunable middle). Equal weights beat optimized weights out of sample more often than people admit.

**Smoothing** — Variance reduction at the cost of lag. EWMA period $n$ implies roughly $(n-1)/2$ bars of lag.

**Filter** — A condition applied after the base signal to suppress trades in hostile environments. Trades frequency for hit-rate.

**Lookahead** — Using information at decision time that wouldn't actually have been available. The single most common cause of unreproducible backtests.

**Time stop** — An exit triggered by elapsed bars. Bounds holding period, which bounds variance.
</Glossary>

## What comes next: Track D

You can now generate signals. The next question is the most uncomfortable in quant finance: **does this signal actually make money?** Track D is about backtesting honestly — return, risk, drawdown, the difference between gross and net, walk-forward validation, and the many ways a beautiful equity curve can be a lie.

If you skipped the workshop (C-09), do it before D-01. The disciplines we will measure with in Track D are the disciplines you practiced in the workshop.

<Pitfall title="The most dangerous moment for a quant">
You have just built a confluence rule that, on five years of SPY, produced a chart you like. The natural conclusion is "I've cracked it." The right conclusion is "I'd better walk-forward test this, on multiple symbols, with realistic costs, before I tell anyone — including myself — that I've cracked anything." The five-years-of-SPY chart is the easiest backtest in the world to fool yourself with.
</Pitfall>

<KeyPoint title="Track C exit ticket">
You have a vocabulary (rule, regime, confluence, filter, aggregation, lookahead) and a process (hypothesis → axes → aggregation → exits → execution → lookahead check → out-of-sample). Track D will teach you to measure honestly what these rules actually do.
</KeyPoint>
