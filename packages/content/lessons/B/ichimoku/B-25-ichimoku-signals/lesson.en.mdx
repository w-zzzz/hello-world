# Ichimoku trading signals: TK cross, Kumo breakout, Chikou clear

The five-line chart is the *state* surface. This lesson is about *transitions* — the events traditional Japanese traders treated as entry triggers. There are three classics: the TK cross, the price-cloud breakout, and the Kumo twist. None is an edge on its own. The edge comes from requiring confluence between them.

## Signal 1: the TK cross

A **TK cross** happens when Tenkan crosses Kijun — structurally similar to a fast-slow MA cross:

$$
\mathrm{TK\ cross}_t = \mathrm{sign}\!\left( \mathrm{Tenkan}_t - \mathrm{Kijun}_t \right) \neq \mathrm{sign}\!\left( \mathrm{Tenkan}_{t-1} - \mathrm{Kijun}_{t-1} \right)
$$

The convention:

- **Bullish TK cross:** Tenkan crosses above Kijun.
- **Bearish TK cross:** Tenkan crosses below Kijun.

Hosoda graded crosses by where they happen relative to the cloud:

- **Strong:** bullish cross *above* the cloud, bearish cross *below*.
- **Neutral:** cross *inside* the cloud.
- **Weak:** bullish cross below the cloud, bearish cross above — fighting the prevailing trend.

## Signal 2: the price-cloud breakout

A **Kumo breakout** is a close that crosses the top or bottom edge of the cloud:

- **Bullish:** close above the top edge (max of SenkouA, SenkouB) after being inside or below.
- **Bearish:** close below the bottom edge after being inside or above.

A breakout through a thin cloud carries more conviction than a poke through a thick one — less aggregated support/resistance to overcome.

## Signal 3: the Kumo twist

A **Kumo twist** is when Senkou A and Senkou B cross *inside the forward-projected cloud*:

$$
\mathrm{Twist}_t \iff \mathrm{sign}\!\left( \mathrm{SenkouA}_t - \mathrm{SenkouB}_t \right) \neq \mathrm{sign}\!\left( \mathrm{SenkouA}_{t-1} - \mathrm{SenkouB}_{t-1} \right)
$$

Because both spans live 26 bars in the future on the chart, you actually see a twist coming — the cloud narrows, pinches, and flips color. Traders read this as a regime change visible *before* it hits current price.

## Confluence: Chikou clear and the full setup

A purist Ichimoku long requires four conditions:

1. Bullish TK cross.
2. Price above the cloud.
3. Cloud ahead is bullish.
4. **Chikou clear** — Chikou is above the price action 26 bars ago, with no candles obstructing it.

Chikou-clear is the check most traders skip and the most useful. It asks: *is today's close better than where this stock was a month ago?* If Chikou is buried inside last month's candles, your "bullish breakout" is into visible supply.

## How the signals compare to plain moving averages

A naive TK cross is *slightly worse* than an EMA(9)/EMA(26) cross on most equity backtests. Tenkan and Kijun use midpoints of highs and lows, not closes. Midpoints are smoother but more sensitive to single-bar wicks. A fair comparison gives:

- TK cross alone: roughly equivalent to a fast/slow MA cross, marginally worse.
- TK cross + cloud filter: meaningfully better — the cloud rejects counter-trend crosses.
- TK cross + cloud + Chikou clear: higher hit rate, lower frequency.

The cross by itself has no Ichimoku-specific edge — it is a moving-average cross with extra steps. The edge is the **filter stack**.

<Pitfall title="Treating the TK cross as the signal">
A TK cross on its own is not an Ichimoku signal — it is a moving-average cross using midpoints. Hosoda's whole point was that the cross is a *trigger* you only act on when the cloud and Chikou agree. Strategies firing on TK crosses alone perform like a noisy fast-slow MA cross and lose the conceptual benefit of the system. If you ablate the cloud and Chikou and the backtest doesn't get visibly worse, you are not actually trading Ichimoku.
</Pitfall>

<KeyPoint title="Signals are events; the cloud is the filter">
TK cross, Kumo breakout, and Kumo twist are the three classic events. The edge comes from confluence: bullish event + price above cloud + bullish cloud ahead + Chikou clear. Remove any of those four and you are back to a marginal MA system.
</KeyPoint>
