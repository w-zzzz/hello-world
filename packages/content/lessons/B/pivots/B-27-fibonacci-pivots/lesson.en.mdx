# Fibonacci pivot levels

Once you understand floor-trader pivots, Fibonacci pivots are a one-line change. Same central pivot, same geometry, same role as session scaffolding — but support and resistance offsets are scaled by Fibonacci ratios instead of integer multipliers. The result is a tighter cluster of levels around the central pivot. In this lesson you will see the formula, compare it to the floor-trader and Camarilla variants, and confront the uncomfortable question of *why* a 0.618 multiple would have any predictive power.

## The formula

Start from the same central pivot:

$$
P = \frac{H + L + C}{3}
$$

Then scale the previous day's range $R = H - L$ by Fibonacci ratios:

$$
R_1 = P + 0.382 \cdot (H - L), \quad S_1 = P - 0.382 \cdot (H - L)
$$

$$
R_2 = P + 0.618 \cdot (H - L), \quad S_2 = P - 0.618 \cdot (H - L)
$$

$$
R_3 = P + 1.000 \cdot (H - L), \quad S_3 = P - 1.000 \cdot (H - L)
$$

The 0.382 and 0.618 are the famous Fibonacci ratios — successive terms of the Fibonacci sequence converge to those numbers. The 1.0 level closes the system at exactly one full range above and below $P$.

## A worked example

Yesterday's SPY: $H = 510$, $L = 504$, $C = 508$, so $H - L = 6$.

$$
P = 507.33
$$

$$
R_1 = 507.33 + 0.382 \cdot 6 = 509.62, \qquad S_1 = 507.33 - 2.29 = 505.04
$$

$$
R_2 = 507.33 + 0.618 \cdot 6 = 511.04, \qquad S_2 = 507.33 - 3.71 = 503.62
$$

$$
R_3 = 507.33 + 6 = 513.33, \qquad S_3 = 507.33 - 6 = 501.33
$$

Fib levels sit *inside* the corresponding floor-trader levels. Floor-trader $R_1$ was $510.67$; Fib $R_1$ is $509.62$. That tighter geometry is the practical difference: Fib pivots fire earlier and more often; floor-trader pivots fire later with bigger moves.

## Comparison to floor-trader and Camarilla pivots

Three families all start from $P = (H+L+C)/3$ but scale differently:

- **Floor-trader:** integer multiples of $H-L$, asymmetric levels. Widest spacing.
- **Fibonacci:** 0.382, 0.618, 1.0 multiples. Medium spacing.
- **Camarilla:** very tight multiples (~1.1/12, 1.1/6 of $H-L$). Smallest spacing — designed for tight mean-reversion.

A confluence trader plots all three. When floor-trader $R_2$, Fib $R_2$, and a Camarilla level land within a few cents, the cluster is treated as a stronger zone.

## The uncomfortable question

If $P$ has a clear interpretation (yesterday's typical price), and floor-trader $R_1$ has one (symmetric reflection of the low across $P$), what does $0.382 \cdot (H-L)$ above $P$ measure?

Honest answer: **nothing intrinsic.** Fibonacci ratios were imported to charts by analogy from natural spirals and the golden ratio, but there is no statistical reason 0.382 or 0.618 should be a special price level. The level matters only because enough chartists and algorithms place orders there. Same sociological edge as floor-trader pivots — except the audience is smaller and more retail-leaning.

<Pitfall title="Confusing pattern with signal">
The Fibonacci ratios feel like deep structure — they show up in spirals, in art, in plant biology. It is tempting to assume they confer real predictive power on price. They do not. If your backtest works only because price respected 0.618, ask: would the same logic with 0.5 or 0.65 also work? If yes, you have a level effect (price respects round-ish numbers in a range), not a Fib effect. If your strategy relies on Fib pivots to confer real edge, you are very likely confusing pattern with signal — the same mistake the system was meant to help you avoid.
</Pitfall>

<KeyPoint title="Same scaffolding, tighter geometry, weaker base">
Fibonacci pivots share the central $P$ with floor-trader pivots but place R/S levels at 0.382, 0.618, 1.0 of yesterday's range — tighter and more frequently touched. The math is just a rescaling. The sociological edge is real but smaller than floor-trader pivots, because the Fib audience is smaller. Use them as descriptive lines, not as oracles.
</KeyPoint>
