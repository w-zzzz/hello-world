# Risk parity

Inverse-volatility weighting balanced each asset's *standalone* risk but ignored how assets move together. Risk parity is the principled fix: it uses the full covariance matrix and sizes positions so that every asset contributes an *equal share* of total portfolio risk. Instead of equal dollars or equal volatility, the goal is equal risk contribution.

This lesson defines risk contribution, states the condition risk parity solves for, and confronts the macro bet it quietly takes on.

## Risk contribution

Total portfolio volatility is $\sigma_p = \sqrt{w^\top \Sigma w}$, where $w$ is the weight vector and $\Sigma$ the covariance matrix. A beautiful property of this formula is that total risk decomposes cleanly across assets. The risk contribution of asset $i$ is:

$$
\mathrm{RC}_i = w_i (\Sigma w)_i, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathrm{RC}_i = w^\top \Sigma w = \sigma_p^2
$$

The term $(\Sigma w)_i$ is asset $i$'s covariance with the whole portfolio, so $\mathrm{RC}_i$ captures not just $i$'s own volatility but how it co-moves with everything else. The contributions sum exactly to total portfolio variance — a true decomposition.

## The risk-parity condition

Risk parity solves for the weights that make all risk contributions equal:

$$
\mathrm{RC}_i = \frac{\sigma_p^2}{N} \quad \text{for all } i
$$

There is no closed-form solution in general, so it is found numerically (an iterative solver). Note that if all assets were uncorrelated, risk parity would collapse exactly to inverse-volatility weighting. Correlation is precisely what makes them differ: when two assets are correlated, risk parity recognizes the overlap and trims both, something inverse-vol cannot do.

## How the weights come out

Because risk parity equalizes risk rather than dollars, low-volatility assets receive *large* dollar weights — often the majority of the book. A classic stock/bond risk-parity portfolio might hold 70-80% in bonds by dollar value, because bonds are so much calmer than stocks that it takes a big dollar allocation for their risk contribution to match equities'.

To then reach an equity-like *return*, practitioners often apply leverage to the whole portfolio. This is the heart of the strategy — and the source of its biggest risk.

<Pitfall title="Risk parity levers up the calm assets">
Risk parity hands large weights to low-volatility assets like bonds, and to chase equity-level returns it often levers the whole book. That worked beautifully through a 40-year bond bull market — but it is a bet that the covariance matrix (especially bond volatility and the stock-bond correlation) stays stable. When rates rise and that correlation flips positive, the levered bond sleeve can turn a defensive book into a painful one.
</Pitfall>

<KeyPoint title="Equalize risk, not dollars">
Risk parity sets weights so every asset's risk contribution RC_i = w_i (Σw)_i is equal, using the full covariance matrix. It diversifies risk genuinely — but it leans on calm, often levered assets and assumes the covariance structure is stable.
</KeyPoint>
