Full fine-tuning of a 70B model needs hundreds of gigabytes of optimizer
state, gradient buffers, and a copy of the weights. LoRA, in 2021, made the
exact same model trainable on a single consumer GPU — by changing what we
update, not what we use.

The idea is so simple it is almost embarrassing. The reason it works is
where the elegance lives.

## Why this matters

Most deployed LLMs are adapted, not pre-trained. Customer-support tone, a
new domain, a safety filter, a refused-style — all of these are tiny
perturbations on top of a base model that already knows English. PEFT
methods exploit that to make adaptation cheap, fast, and composable.

## The core observation

For a pre-trained linear layer $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, the *update*
$\Delta W$ produced by fine-tuning empirically has very low effective rank.
LoRA encodes that prior into the parameterization:

$$
W' = W + \Delta W, \qquad \Delta W = B A, \quad A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}, \; B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}
$$

Freeze $W$. Train only $A$ and $B$. With $r$ as small as 4 or 8, you cover
most of the useful adaptation directions while updating $\sim 2rd$ instead
of $d^2$ parameters.

<Callout variant="insight" title="Why low rank is plausible">
A pre-trained model already has rich features; what fine-tuning needs to
do is rotate or rescale a small subspace of them. The intrinsic dimension
of "make this model polite" is probably tens to hundreds, not millions.
</Callout>

## The math

Initialize $A$ with a small Gaussian and $B = 0$ so the adapter contributes
zero at step 0. The forward becomes

$$
y = W x + \alpha \cdot B A x
$$

where $\alpha/r$ is a fixed scaling factor that decouples the LoRA rank
from the effective learning rate. Both $A$ and $B$ are trained; gradients
flow only into them.

```python
import torch.nn as nn

class LoRALinear(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, base: nn.Linear, r=8, alpha=16):
        super().__init__()
        self.base = base
        for p in self.base.parameters():
            p.requires_grad_(False)
        d_out, d_in = base.weight.shape
        self.A = nn.Parameter(torch.randn(r, d_in) * 0.01)
        self.B = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(d_out, r))
        self.scale = alpha / r

    def forward(self, x):
        return self.base(x) + self.scale * (x @ self.A.T) @ self.B.T
```

In practice you wrap the $W_Q$, $W_V$, sometimes $W_O$ and MLP projections —
not every linear layer. The Hugging Face `peft` library does this by name
match.

## QLoRA: the trick that put it on a laptop

QLoRA freezes the base model in **NF4**, a 4-bit data type whose levels are
spaced by quantiles of a normal distribution (so they match the actual
weight distribution rather than uniform spacing). The adapter weights stay
in bf16. Two extras are decisive in practice:

1. **Double quantization** — quantize the per-block scaling factors
   themselves, saving another ~0.4 bits per weight.
2. **Paged optimizers** — when the optimizer's transient state would OOM,
   page it to CPU memory.

The combination is what makes 65B-parameter QLoRA fine-tuning fit in 48 GB.

## Practical knobs

- **Rank $r$**: 4–16 covers most tasks. Larger $r$ helps when adaptation is
  not just stylistic.
- **$\alpha$**: $\alpha/r \approx 1{-}2$. People debate this; just sweep.
- **Targets**: at minimum $W_Q, W_V$. Adding MLP projections (DoRA, full
  PEFT) helps for harder shifts.
- **Learning rate**: $1\text{e-}4$ to $5\text{e-}4$ — much higher than
  full fine-tuning's $1\text{e-}5$.

<Callout variant="aside" title="Adapter zoo">
LoRA is one point in a much larger space: prompt tuning, prefix tuning,
$(\text{IA})^3$, BitFit, AdaLoRA, DoRA, VeRA. Each pushes a different
trade-off between expressivity, memory, and merge-ability. PEFT exposes
them behind a common config.
</Callout>

## When LoRA is the wrong tool

Pretraining-style distribution shift (new language, new modality,
unlocking entirely new capabilities) often needs full fine-tuning or a
higher-rank adapter. LoRA shines for *behavioral* changes; it does not
manufacture knowledge that the base model lacks.

Treat LoRA as the default. Reach for full fine-tuning when adapters
plateau and the underlying base genuinely needs more capacity.
