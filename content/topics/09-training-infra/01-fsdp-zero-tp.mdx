A 70B-parameter Transformer in float16 weights alone is 140 GB — already past
any single accelerator. Add gradients, Adam's two moment buffers, and
activations, and a naive setup needs roughly **16 bytes per parameter** of
state. The only reason this fits on a few GPUs is a small zoo of
parallelism strategies, all of which boil down to: *don't keep everything
everywhere*.

## Why this matters

The constraints on training large models are not algorithmic — they are
memory and bandwidth. Whether you can train a model at all, how fast a step
runs, and what utilization you achieve all hinge on which dimensions of work
you split, across what topology, and which collectives that requires.

Understanding the four primitives — DP, ZeRO, TP, PP — is what separates
"can launch a training job" from "can debug why the loss is flat at 1024
GPUs."

## Data parallelism (DDP) and its ceiling

Plain DDP replicates the full model on every rank, splits the batch, and
all-reduces gradients before the optimizer step. It is dead simple and
scales near-linearly in throughput, but every rank holds the full state:

$$
\text{mem}_\text{DDP} \approx \underbrace{2P}_\text{weights} + \underbrace{2P}_\text{grads} + \underbrace{12P}_\text{Adam fp32}
$$

For a 70B model in mixed precision, that's ~1.1 TB per rank — completely
infeasible.

## ZeRO and FSDP: shard the state

ZeRO's insight is that a rank does not need optimizer state for parameters
it isn't currently updating. The three stages partition more aggressively:

- **ZeRO-1**: shard *optimizer states* across DP ranks.
- **ZeRO-2**: also shard *gradients*.
- **ZeRO-3** (= PyTorch FSDP): also shard *parameters*. Each layer's weights
  are all-gathered just before its forward, used, and immediately freed.

The cost is more communication — an extra all-gather per forward and
backward — but with overlap, modern hardware hides most of it.

<Callout variant="insight" title="The right mental model">
ZeRO/FSDP turn the GPU cluster into one giant memory pool that you stream
layer-by-layer through compute. You pay communication for memory; the
cheaper your interconnect, the worse this trade.
</Callout>

## Tensor parallelism: split a matmul

For a linear $y = xW$ with $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d'}$, you can split
$W$ column-wise across $t$ devices and compute partial outputs in parallel.
Megatron's MLP block uses **column-parallel** followed by **row-parallel**,
needing a single all-reduce per block. Attention is the same idea on the
$QKV$ projection.

TP requires per-layer all-reduces, so it is bandwidth-hungry and lives
*inside* a single NVLink-connected node (typically $t = 8$).

## Pipeline parallelism: split layers across stages

PP partitions the *layer stack* across devices, like a factory line. Without
care, only one stage works at a time. GPipe / 1F1B schedules use
micro-batches to keep stages busy:

```python
# 1F1B-style schedule (sketch)
for step in range(num_steps):
    for mb in micro_batches:
        fwd_in = recv_from_prev()
        fwd_out = stage.forward(fwd_in)
        send_to_next(fwd_out)
    for mb in reversed(micro_batches):
        bwd_in = recv_from_next()
        bwd_out = stage.backward(bwd_in)
        send_to_prev(bwd_out)
    optimizer.step()
```

The remaining "bubble" is the unavoidable warmup and cooldown of the
pipeline.

## 3D parallelism in practice

Frontier training stacks (Megatron-LM, DeepSpeed, NeMo, MaxText) compose all
three: TP within a node, PP across nodes for very deep models, DP wrapping
the whole thing. A 70B model on 64 GPUs might use TP=8, PP=4, DP=2.

The art is choosing the split so that no collective is a bottleneck and the
arithmetic intensity stays high. As a rough rule: prefer TP over PP when you
have NVLink, prefer PP over TP when you don't, and let FSDP handle whatever
DP layer wraps it.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Throughput is not utilization">
A job that scales to 1024 GPUs at 30% MFU (model FLOPs utilization) is
wasting the same money as a smaller job at 90%. Always look at MFU, not just
samples/sec, and profile the actual collective overlap before claiming
linear scaling.
</Callout>
