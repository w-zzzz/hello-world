In 2017, eight Google researchers published a paper with one of the more
audacious titles in deep learning history. They were right. Attention turned
out to be enough — it killed RNNs for language modeling, then it killed
purpose-built CNNs for vision, then it became the foundation of essentially
every frontier AI system.

Attention is conceptually simple. Walking through it carefully will pay back
dividends for the rest of the curriculum.

## The one idea

Given a sequence of vectors $x_1, \dots, x_n$, you want each position to be
able to *look at* every other position and pull in whatever information is
useful, weighted by relevance.

Concretely: project each token into three vectors — a **query** $q_i$ (what am
I looking for?), a **key** $k_j$ (what am I about?), and a **value** $v_j$
(what should I send if you pick me?). Then for each query, take a weighted sum
of values where the weights come from query–key similarity:

$$
\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V
$$

That's the entire mechanism. Everything downstream — Transformers, BERT, GPT,
diffusion text-encoders, video models — is some arrangement of this primitive
plus MLPs, residuals, and normalization.

## Why query–key–value

You could imagine cheaper alternatives. Why not just take a learned weighted
average of all token vectors? Because the right weighting depends on the
*content* of the tokens. The token "it" in *"the trophy didn't fit in the
suitcase because it was too big"* should attend to "trophy", but a different
sentence demands a different attendee. Static weights cannot do this.

QKV factors the relevance computation into:

- **Q** and **K** decide *who attends to whom* — the attention map.
- **V** decides *what gets passed* once the routing is fixed.

This separation is what lets the same architecture handle wildly different
tasks: copy, retrieval, induction, in-context learning. Different patterns
correspond to different attention maps.

## The math, in three steps

For one head with input $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$:

1. **Project**: $Q = XW_Q$, $K = XW_K$, $V = XW_V$, with $W_Q, W_K \in
   \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_k}$ and $W_V \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d_v}$.

2. **Score and normalize**:

   $$
   A = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)
   $$

   $A_{ij}$ is the attention weight from query $i$ to key $j$.

3. **Aggregate**: $\text{out} = AV$. Each row of $\text{out}$ is a convex
   combination of the value vectors weighted by attention.

The $\sqrt{d_k}$ scaling deserves attention (no pun). If $Q$ and $K$ have
unit-variance entries, the dot product $q_i \cdot k_j$ has variance about
$d_k$. Large logits push softmax into regions where the gradient is nearly
zero — saturating softmax is a backprop killer. Scaling restores well-behaved
gradients regardless of $d_k$.

## Multi-head: many small attentions are better than one big one

Instead of one attention head with $d_k = d$, the Transformer uses $h$ heads
with $d_k = d/h$ in parallel. Each head has its own $W_Q, W_K, W_V$, computes
its own attention, and the results are concatenated and re-projected:

$$
\text{MHA}(X) = [\text{head}_1; \dots; \text{head}_h] W_O
$$

```python
import torch.nn.functional as F

def attention(Q, K, V, mask=None):
    # Q, K, V: (B, H, T, d_k)
    scores = (Q @ K.transpose(-2, -1)) / (Q.size(-1) ** 0.5)
    if mask is not None:
        scores = scores.masked_fill(mask == 0, float("-inf"))
    weights = F.softmax(scores, dim=-1)
    return weights @ V, weights
```

Heads specialize. Empirical interpretability work has identified heads that
track grammatical agreement, copy nearby tokens (induction heads), look up the
previous occurrence of a token, and many more.

## Causal masking and parallel training

In a decoder (the GPT-style architecture you'll see most often), each token
should be able to attend only to itself and previous tokens — never the
future. The implementation: zero out the upper-triangular entries of the
score matrix before the softmax. Because the mask is fixed, the entire
sequence can be processed in parallel during training, even though the model
is autoregressive at inference time.

This is the property that made Transformers so much faster to train than RNNs:
the recurrence is removed in *training*, even though the *generation* is
still left-to-right.

## Positional information

Because attention is permutation-equivariant, the model has no built-in notion
of order. Positional encodings inject it. The original paper used sinusoids
of different frequencies. Modern variants (RoPE, ALiBi) encode position
directly into the QK product, which generalizes more cleanly to longer
contexts than were seen at training time.

<Callout variant="insight" title="Reading attention with humility">
The intuition that "head $h$ attends from token $i$ to token $j$, therefore
$j$ caused $i$'s representation" is appealing but often wrong. Attention
weights are necessary but not sufficient evidence of causal influence —
information also flows through residual streams and MLPs. Mech-interp work
(circuits, SAEs) gives a more honest picture.
</Callout>
