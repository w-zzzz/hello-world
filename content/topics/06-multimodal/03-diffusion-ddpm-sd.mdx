Diffusion is the dominant paradigm for generating images, video, audio, 3D
structure, and increasingly molecules and proteins. It's also one of the
most beautiful ideas in deep learning: instead of learning to *generate*
data directly, learn to *denoise* it. Generation becomes the inversion of
a fixed Gaussian noising process.

The 2020 DDPM paper turned a textbook idea (Sohl-Dickstein 2015) into a
practical recipe. The 2022 Stable Diffusion paper made it scale. By 2024,
Stable Diffusion 3, FLUX, Imagen 3, and Sora had pushed diffusion-based
generation to photorealism.

## The forward process: a fixed Markov chain to noise

Define a sequence of latents $x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_T$ where $x_0$ is the
clean image and each step adds a small amount of Gaussian noise:

$$
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1}) = \mathcal{N}\!\big(x_t;\ \sqrt{1-\beta_t}\, x_{t-1},\ \beta_t I\big)
$$

The schedule $\beta_t$ is fixed — a small linear or cosine ramp. After
enough steps (DDPM used $T=1000$), the data is washed out: $x_T \approx
\mathcal{N}(0, I)$.

A useful identity: thanks to the Gaussian closed form, you can sample
$x_t$ from $x_0$ in one shot:

$$
x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}\, x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\, \epsilon,\quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)
$$

where $\bar{\alpha}_t = \prod_{s=1}^t (1-\beta_s)$. This is what makes
training tractable — no need to actually run $t$ noising steps.

## The reverse process: a learned denoiser

The whole game is to *invert* the forward chain. Parameterize a neural
network $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)$ to predict the noise that was added. Then
the loss is gloriously simple:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x_0, t, \epsilon}\, \big\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\big\|^2
$$

Sample a clean image $x_0$, sample a timestep $t \in \{1, \ldots, T\}$,
sample noise $\epsilon$, form $x_t$, and ask the network to recover
$\epsilon$. That's it. No adversarial game, no variational bound to wrestle
with — just MSE on noise.

The deep claim: this MSE objective is (up to per-timestep weighting) a
valid lower bound on the data likelihood. Generation by denoising is
*principled*, not just empirical.

```python
import torch, torch.nn.functional as F

def diffusion_loss(model, x0, betas):
    B = x0.size(0)
    T = len(betas)
    alpha_bars = torch.cumprod(1 - betas, dim=0)
    t = torch.randint(0, T, (B,), device=x0.device)
    a_bar = alpha_bars[t].view(-1, 1, 1, 1)
    eps = torch.randn_like(x0)
    x_t = a_bar.sqrt() * x0 + (1 - a_bar).sqrt() * eps
    eps_pred = model(x_t, t)
    return F.mse_loss(eps_pred, eps)
```

That's the training loop. The architecture (U-Net or DiT), the timestep
embedding, the conditioning — all secondary.

## Sampling: walking back from noise

To generate, start from $x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and iteratively denoise:

$$
x_{t-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta_t}}\Big(x_t - \frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}} \epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)\Big) + \sigma_t z
$$

with $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ for $t > 1$ and $\sigma_t = \sqrt{\beta_t}$
(or another choice). 1000 steps was DDPM's original schedule. Modern
samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, Euler, Heun) can produce equivalent quality in
20–50 steps by treating the reverse process as an SDE/ODE and using
higher-order numerical integrators.

## Latent diffusion: Stable Diffusion's contribution

Pixel-space diffusion at 512×512 is brutally expensive. Rombach et al.
(2022) made the key engineering move: train a VAE that compresses
512×512×3 images into 64×64×4 latents (a ~48× reduction), and run the
diffusion model in *latent space*. Decode the final clean latent through
the VAE to get pixels.

This works because the VAE's lossy compression discards perceptually
unimportant details — exactly the details that diffusion models waste
capacity on. The result: open-weights, high-resolution, fast,
text-conditioned image generation. Stable Diffusion in 2022 was the
moment generative AI became mainstream.

Conditioning enters via cross-attention: the text encoder (CLIP, T5)
produces token embeddings, and U-Net layers attend to them at multiple
resolutions.

## Classifier-free guidance

A practical trick that dominates real samplers. During training, randomly
drop the conditioning (replace text embedding with a null embedding) 10%
of the time. The model now jointly learns conditional and unconditional
denoising.

At sampling, *extrapolate*:

$$
\hat{\epsilon} = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) + w\,\big(\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \emptyset)\big)
$$

For $w = 0$, you get the conditional model. For $w > 0$, you push the
denoising direction *away from unconditional* and *toward conditional*,
which sharpens the conditional distribution. Most users never see this
machinery — they see a "guidance scale" slider, often defaulting to ~7.5.

## What changed in 2023–2026

- **DiT** (Diffusion Transformers, Peebles & Xie 2022): replaced U-Net
  with a Transformer backbone. Cleaner scaling laws. Sora and SD3 are
  DiTs.
- **Rectified flow / flow matching** (next topic): replaced the SDE
  formulation with an ODE that learns straight-line trajectories,
  enabling fewer-step (sometimes one-step) sampling.
- **Stable Diffusion 3 / FLUX**: rectified-flow DiTs with multiple text
  encoders (T5 + CLIP) and 8B+ parameters. Photorealistic, prompt-faithful.
- **Sora, Veo, Gen-3** (covered later): video diffusion at scale.
- **Distillation**: turning a 50-step diffusion model into a 1-4 step
  model via consistency models, LCMs, or adversarial distillation.

<Callout variant="insight" title="Why diffusion beat GANs">
GANs were the dominant generative model for years and remained competitive
on quality, but lost on three axes: (1) training stability — diffusion
just works, GANs need careful hyperparameter tuning; (2) coverage —
diffusion captures the full distribution, GANs collapse modes; (3)
controllability — diffusion conditions cleanly via cross-attention, GANs
were more brittle. The shift was decisive by ~2022.
</Callout>
