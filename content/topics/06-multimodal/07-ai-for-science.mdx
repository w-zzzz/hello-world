The most consequential AI deployments of the last five years were not chat
products. They were AlphaFold 2, AlphaFold 3, GraphCast, AlphaProof, and
their open-source successors. These systems solved long-standing scientific
problems and changed how working scientists do their day-to-day jobs. They
deserve a place in any modern ML curriculum, both because they are
intellectually beautiful and because they are templates for the next wave.

This module is a tour. Five projects, what each got right, and what to read
next.

## AlphaFold 2: the protein-folding decade ends

The protein folding problem is fifty years old: given an amino-acid sequence,
predict the 3D structure the protein folds into. Until 2020 it was an
unsolved problem; the best methods scored ~50 GDT_TS on the CASP critical
assessment. AlphaFold 2 (DeepMind, 2020-2021) scored 92 — competitive with
experimental crystallography for many targets — and the field declared the
problem effectively solved.

The technical recipe:

1. **Multiple sequence alignment (MSA)** as input. Co-evolution of residues
   across organisms is the strongest signal for which residues touch in 3D.
2. **Evoformer blocks** that jointly update the MSA representation
   (rows = sequences, columns = residues) and a pair representation (one
   embedding per residue pair). Attention runs along rows, columns, and
   pairs.
3. **Structure module** that emits 3D coordinates as a sequence of
   rigid-body frames per residue, trained with a frame-aligned point error
   that respects equivariance.
4. **Recycling** through the network 3-4 times so the structure prediction
   can refine the MSA representation iteratively.

The DeepMind/EBI AlphaFold Database now hosts predicted structures for
~200M proteins — essentially every known sequence on Earth.

## AlphaFold 3: from proteins to all of life's molecules

AF3 (2024) generalizes from "predict structure of a single protein" to
"predict structure of an arbitrary biomolecular complex" — proteins +
ligands + nucleic acids + ions, all together. The architectural change is
significant: the AF2 frame-based structure module is replaced by a
**diffusion** module that denoises atom coordinates. Inputs are now any
combination of biomolecule types, encoded into a unified representation.

The practical impact: drug-target interaction prediction (where you give
the model a candidate small molecule plus a target protein and get back
the predicted binding pose) is a single forward pass. This is one of
the most expensive operations in computational drug discovery, and AF3
brought it within reach of any lab with a few GPUs.

The catch: AF3 was released as a hosted server with restricted commercial
use. **Boltz-1** (MIT, late 2024) is a clean-room open-source competitor
released under MIT license. It is roughly comparable in accuracy and is
already the default for academic use.

## GraphCast: weather without a supercomputer

Numerical weather prediction (NWP) — solving the equations of atmospheric
fluid dynamics on a 3D grid — is one of the largest scientific computations
in the world. ECMWF's HRES model runs on tens of thousands of CPUs to
produce a 10-day global forecast.

GraphCast (DeepMind, 2023) replaces the PDE solver with a graph neural
network on an icosahedral mesh. Trained on 39 years of ERA5 reanalysis
data, it autoregressively predicts 6-hour increments. On most surface and
upper-air variables it beats ECMWF's HRES at 10-day lead, and it runs in
about a minute on one TPU.

GenCast (2024) adds ensemble forecasting via diffusion sampling.
Microsoft's Aurora and NVIDIA's FourCastNet (Anandkumar's group) are
parallel efforts using Fourier neural operators. The field has effectively
re-implemented numerical weather prediction in pure ML, several orders of
magnitude faster.

## AlphaProof / AlphaGeometry 2: silver at the IMO

In July 2024 DeepMind announced that AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2,
working together, scored 28/42 on the 2024 IMO problems — silver-medal
level, one point below the gold cutoff. The recipe was *not* "scale up an
LLM." It was a neuro-symbolic loop:

- **AlphaProof** uses an LLM to propose proof steps in **Lean**, the formal
  theorem prover. Lean verifies each step. The valid-proof reward is then
  used in self-play RL across millions of synthetic problems.
- **AlphaGeometry 2** combines an LLM with a symbolic geometry engine that
  performs auxiliary-construction search and verification.

This is the cleanest existing template for *verifier-grounded reasoning RL*.
The LLM proposes; the verifier scores; RL closes the loop. Apply the same
pattern to code (with tests as the verifier), to physics simulations, to
formal protocols — and you have the next generation of reasoning systems.

```python
# Conceptual loop. The verifier is the magic — it provides perfect rewards.
while not solved(problem):
    candidate = llm.propose_step(state, history)  # natural-ish language
    formal    = translate_to_lean(candidate)      # parser
    ok, msg   = lean.check(formal)                # verifier
    state     = state + (formal if ok else None)
    reward    = +1 if proof_complete(state) else 0
    rl.update(llm, candidate, reward)
```

## Why this matters

The pattern across all four:

- **Strong inductive biases** (MSA + pair attention, icosahedral graph,
  Lean type system) instead of pure scale.
- **Domain-specific evaluation** with ground truth (CASP, ERA5, IMO
  problems) — verifiable rewards.
- **Self-play / synthetic data** at enormous scale once the verifier is
  in place.

This recipe — domain inductive bias + verifiable reward + scale — is
arguably more important to the future of ML than chat-product
benchmarks. It is what produces *new science* rather than recapitulating
what is already on the web.

## What to do next

- Run AlphaFold 3 (or Boltz-1, if you want open-source) on a sequence you
  care about. It will take five minutes and reshape your sense of what
  ML can do.
- Read GraphCast carefully — it is one of the cleanest examples of GNN +
  scientific simulation in the literature.
- Read the AlphaProof blog post and skim the Lean proof traces. The
  combination of LLM proposal and formal verification is the most
  important emerging RL recipe of 2025-26.
