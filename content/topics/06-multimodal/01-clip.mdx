CLIP is the architecture that taught computers to *understand* images by
reading captions. Until 2021, computer vision lived in a 1000-class
ImageNet world: a model could tell a Persian cat from a Siamese cat, but it
had no idea what "Persian" or "cat" *meant*. CLIP shattered that ceiling by
training a single model on 400 million internet image-text pairs with a
contrastive objective. The result was a model that could classify, retrieve,
caption, and ground language to vision — all without task-specific
fine-tuning.

Half of modern multimodal AI runs on CLIP or its descendants. Understanding
the contrastive recipe is the foundation for LLaVA, Stable Diffusion's text
conditioning, and most vision-language evaluation.

## The setup

CLIP has two towers:

- An **image encoder** $f_{\text{img}}$ — originally a ResNet, later ViT —
  that maps an image to a vector $u \in \mathbb{R}^d$.
- A **text encoder** $f_{\text{txt}}$ — a Transformer — that maps a caption
  to a vector $v \in \mathbb{R}^d$.

Both vectors are L2-normalized. For a matched (image, caption) pair, we
want $u \cdot v$ large; for a mismatched pair, small.

## Symmetric InfoNCE

Given a batch of $N$ pairs $\{(x_i, t_i)\}$, encode them to
$\{(u_i, v_i)\}$ and form the $N \times N$ similarity matrix
$S_{ij} = u_i^\top v_j / \tau$ where $\tau$ is a learned temperature. The
loss is symmetric cross-entropy over rows and columns:

$$
\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[ \log \frac{e^{S_{ii}}}{\sum_j e^{S_{ij}}} + \log \frac{e^{S_{ii}}}{\sum_j e^{S_{ji}}} \Big]
$$

The first term: each image should pick its true caption out of the $N$
candidates. The second term: each caption should pick its true image. The
$N^2 - N$ mismatched pairs are the negatives — no explicit sampling needed.

The temperature $\tau$ matters. Too large and softmax flattens, gradients
vanish. Too small and softmax saturates. CLIP learns it.

```python
import torch, torch.nn.functional as F

def clip_loss(image_emb, text_emb, temperature):
    # both are (B, d) and L2-normalized
    logits = (image_emb @ text_emb.T) / temperature  # (B, B)
    targets = torch.arange(len(logits), device=logits.device)
    loss_i = F.cross_entropy(logits, targets)        # row-wise
    loss_t = F.cross_entropy(logits.T, targets)      # col-wise
    return (loss_i + loss_t) / 2
```

That's the entire training objective.

## Why contrastive over generative

You could imagine training a generative caption model — given an image,
maximize $\log p(\text{caption} \mid \text{image})$. People did. CLIP's
authors argued it was the wrong abstraction. Captions are ambiguous:
"dog" and "puppy" and "Golden Retriever" might all be correct for the same
image. Generative training wastes capacity on surface form.

Contrastive training only cares whether the image and caption are *the
matched pair* in this batch. It throws away the linguistic detail in favor
of *alignment*. This turned out to be both faster (an order of magnitude)
and better at the resulting downstream tasks.

## Zero-shot classification

The cleanest CLIP demo: classify ImageNet without ever training on
ImageNet labels.

1. Take the 1000 class names.
2. Embed each as text: *"a photo of a \{class\}"* (the prompt template
   matters; CLIP authors found small wins from ensembling templates).
3. For a test image, compute its embedding.
4. Predict the class whose text embedding has highest cosine similarity.

CLIP-ViT-L/14 reached ~76% top-1 on ImageNet zero-shot — competitive with
fully supervised ResNets from a few years prior, with no ImageNet
supervision at all.

## Where CLIP sits in modern systems

- **Stable Diffusion 1.x** uses CLIP's text encoder to condition image
  generation. SD 2/3 use OpenCLIP and T5.
- **LLaVA** and most open VLMs feed CLIP-ViT image patches into an LLM as
  visual tokens.
- **DALL-E 2** uses CLIP embeddings as the bridge between text and image
  diffusion.
- **Image search** at scale runs on CLIP-style embeddings.
- **CLIPScore** is the standard automatic metric for image-caption
  alignment.

Few 2021 papers had this much downstream impact. The contrastive recipe
turned out to generalize — to audio (CLAP), 3D, video, even protein
sequences.

<Callout variant="insight" title="The data, not the loss">
CLIP's loss is a 5-line idea. Its dataset — 400M (image, caption) pairs
scraped from the web — was the actually new thing. LAION later released a
2B-pair public dataset (LAION-2B) and OpenCLIP reproductions matched or
exceeded CLIP. The recipe scales with data; the loss is incidental.
</Callout>

## SigLIP and what came next

The 2023 SigLIP paper replaced CLIP's batch-wide softmax with a
per-pair sigmoid loss. Each (image, text) pair becomes an independent
binary classification: "match or not?" This eliminates the all-to-all
dependency in the loss, which lets you shard much larger batches across
GPUs without all-gather communication. SigLIP and SigLIP 2 are now the
default image encoders for most open VLMs (PaliGemma, Idefics, recent
LLaVA variants).

The contrastive paradigm is settling into a stable engineering recipe.
The action has moved on — to generative multimodal models (next topics) —
but the embeddings produced by CLIP/SigLIP-style training remain the
substrate underneath them.
