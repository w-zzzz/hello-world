If diffusion was the dominant idea of 2020–2023, **flow matching** is its
2024–2026 cleanup. Same generative paradigm — transport noise to data —
with a cleaner mathematical formulation, simpler loss, and trajectories
that can often be integrated in 1–4 steps instead of 30–50. Stable
Diffusion 3 and FLUX are flow-matching models. So is Meta's Movie Gen.
The shift is quiet but pervasive.

You don't need to abandon diffusion intuition to learn flow matching — the
two are deeply related. But the new framing makes some things obvious that
were obscure before, and (it turns out) yields better-behaved models.

## The continuous picture

Both diffusion and flow matching describe a *continuous-time* path of
distributions $\{p_t\}_{t \in [0,1]}$ that smoothly interpolates between a
simple prior $p_0$ (typically Gaussian) and the data distribution $p_1$.

The two paradigms differ in *how* they characterize and learn this path:

- **Score-based / diffusion**: learn the *score* $\nabla_x \log p_t(x)$ at
  each timestep. Generation is reverse-time SDE integration.
- **Flow matching**: learn a *vector field* $v_t(x)$ such that integrating
  $\dot{x}_t = v_t(x_t)$ from $x_0$ produces a sample from $p_t$.
  Generation is forward-time ODE integration.

The two are related: any score function induces a probability-flow ODE,
and any flow can be interpreted probabilistically. But the *training
objective* and the *sampling dynamics* differ.

## The flow-matching objective

The clean idea: pick any "interpolant" $x_t = \alpha_t x_0 + \sigma_t x_1$
where $x_0 \sim p_{\text{noise}}$ and $x_1 \sim p_{\text{data}}$ are
independent. The conditional velocity along this path is

$$
u_t(x \mid x_0, x_1) = \dot{\alpha}_t x_0 + \dot{\sigma}_t x_1
$$

a known closed form. Train a network $v_\theta(x, t)$ to match it:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1}\, \big\| v_\theta(x_t, t) - u_t(x \mid x_0, x_1) \big\|^2
$$

The miracle (proved in Lipman et al. 2023): regressing on the *conditional*
velocity matches the *marginal* velocity in expectation. So this trains
the right vector field without ever needing access to the marginal.

```python
def fm_loss(model, x1):
    B = x1.size(0)
    x0 = torch.randn_like(x1)
    t  = torch.rand(B, device=x1.device).view(-1, *([1]*(x1.ndim-1)))
    xt = (1 - t) * x0 + t * x1                # linear interpolation
    target_v = x1 - x0                         # constant velocity
    pred_v = model(xt, t.squeeze())
    return F.mse_loss(pred_v, target_v)
```

Three lines for the loss. Compare DDPM's noise-prediction loss — it
reduces to nearly the same form, but flow matching makes the geometric
intuition explicit.

## Rectified flow: prefer straight lines

The simplest interpolation $x_t = (1-t) x_0 + t x_1$ — a *linear*
trajectory — is the heart of **rectified flow** (Liu et al., 2022). Two
useful properties follow:

1. The target velocity $u_t = x_1 - x_0$ is *constant in $t$*. The model is
   trained to predict the same direction at every point along the path.
2. If the model perfectly fit constant velocities, sampling would require
   only *one* Euler step: $x_1 = x_0 + v_\theta(x_0, 0)$.

In practice the model can't perfectly recover constant velocities (because
of crossings — many noise samples can lead to the same data sample), so
trajectories curve. But Liu et al. introduced a beautiful recursive
*reflow* procedure: re-pair the noise samples with their model-generated
endpoints, retrain. After a couple of reflows, trajectories become nearly
straight, enabling few-step sampling at full quality.

## Why SD3 and FLUX picked rectified flow

Esser et al. (2024, the SD3 paper) ran perhaps the most systematic
empirical comparison of generative objectives at scale: eps-prediction
(DDPM), v-prediction, score-matching, EDM, rectified flow, and several
hybrids. Their findings:

- **Rectified flow consistently won** on FID and text alignment across
  all model sizes they tested (450M–8B parameters).
- **Logit-normal timestep weighting** — sample $t$ from a distribution
  that focuses on middle timesteps where the loss carries the most signal
  — gave another consistent boost.
- The combination scaled cleanly with parameters and compute, with no
  surprise pathologies.

This is why SD3, FLUX, and most 2024–2026 frontier image and video models
are rectified-flow Transformers (DiTs). The architecture is shared with
diffusion; the training objective changed.

## Connection to optimal transport

If you squint, flow matching is doing something close to *dynamic optimal
transport*: finding a vector field that transports $p_0$ to $p_1$ with
minimal kinetic cost. Linear interpolants give exact OT trajectories
*conditional on a pairing*; the model learns the marginal vector field
that best approximates this. This is one of the threads tying generative
modeling to a much older mathematical literature on Monge and
Kantorovich.

<Callout variant="insight" title="Same algorithm, cleaner story">
Flow matching is not a fundamentally different generative paradigm from
diffusion. It is a re-derivation that makes the geometry obvious, the loss
simpler, and (with rectification) the sampling faster. Most of the
practical gains of 2024 image and video generation come from this cleanup,
plus DiT backbones, plus better data.
</Callout>

## Where the action is now

- **Few-step generation**: rectified flow + adversarial distillation
  (FLUX.1-schnell) gives high-quality 1-4 step sampling.
- **Consistency models** (Song et al.): an alternative path to 1-step
  sampling via direct boundary-condition learning.
- **Conditional flow matching** for non-image domains: protein backbones,
  molecule geometries, point clouds.
- **Discrete flow matching**: extending the framework to discrete
  modalities (text, code) — an active 2025 research direction.

If diffusion is the workhorse of generative modeling, flow matching is the
chassis being refactored beneath it. Most of what you'll build with in
2026 is a flow-matching model whether you label it that way or not.
