Video generation went from "amusing 2-second loops" to "minute-long
photorealistic clips with audio" in roughly 18 months, and the architectural
recipe stabilized faster than anyone expected. Sora (announced February
2024), Runway Gen-3 Alpha, Kling, Luma Dream Machine, Pika, Veo 2, Veo 3,
Meta Movie Gen, HunyuanVideo, Mochi, CogVideoX — all use variants of the
same core pattern: **a video VAE compresses spacetime into latent patches,
and a diffusion Transformer denoises them**.

This module is short because the core idea is small. The interesting
content is in what the move from images to video forces you to confront.

## Spacetime patches: video as a token sequence

The cleanest framing comes from Sora's technical report. A video is a
3D tensor — height × width × time, plus channels. Cut it into small 3D
cubes (e.g., 2 consecutive frames × 16 × 16 pixels), pass each through a
*causal 3D VAE encoder*, and you get a sequence of latent **spacetime
patches**. Flatten to a 1D sequence and you have a token stream the same
shape a Transformer expects.

The DiT then denoises this token stream. Conditioning (text prompts,
sometimes images, sometimes audio) enters via cross-attention or via
tokens prepended to the sequence. The recipe is essentially "Stable
Diffusion 3, but now your tokens are spacetime cubes."

Two consequences fall out of this design:

1. **Variable resolution and duration**. Because the model sees a sequence
   of patches, you can train on (and generate) videos of arbitrary
   spatial resolution and temporal length, up to memory limits. This is
   how Sora handles 1920×1080 *and* 1080×1920 *and* square crops with one
   model.
2. **Native temporal consistency**. The DiT attends across all spacetime
   patches simultaneously, so a character's face on frame 1 can directly
   inform the same face on frame 60 via self-attention. This is much
   stronger than older "image diffusion + temporal layers" hacks.

## Why the video VAE matters so much

Pixel-space diffusion is hopeless for video. A 5-second 1080p clip at 24fps
is roughly $120 \times 1920 \times 1080 \times 3 \approx 7.5 \times 10^8$
pixel values. You cannot do 30 denoising forward passes on that volume.

A causal 3D VAE compresses by perhaps 4× spatially in each dimension and
4–8× temporally — call it 100× total. The latent video is now ~$10^7$
values, which is on the order of a single high-resolution image. *Now*
diffusion is tractable.

"Causal" in the temporal dimension means each compressed latent only
depends on past frames, which lets the VAE be applied online to streaming
video. The spatial compression is shared with the standard image VAE
recipe.

```python
# Pseudo-pipeline for a Sora-style video model
# 1. Compress
latents = video_vae.encode(video)           # (B, C, T', H', W'),  T'=T/4, H'=H/8, W'=W/8
# 2. Patchify into spacetime tokens
tokens = patchify_3d(latents, p=(2,2,2))    # (B, N_tokens, D)
# 3. DiT denoising in latent space
denoised = dit(noisy_tokens, t, text_emb)   # rectified-flow or eps-prediction
# 4. Unpatchify, decode
video_out = video_vae.decode(unpatchify(denoised))
```

This is also exactly the pattern HunyuanVideo and Mochi-1 publish.

## What video forced researchers to confront

Image diffusion glosses over a few things that video makes painfully
explicit:

- **Object permanence**: an object that leaves the frame should reappear
  consistently. Pure feedforward DiTs eventually fail at this for long
  clips; the field is exploring memory mechanisms (KV-cache reuse,
  compressed memory tokens, latent state recurrence).
- **Physics**: cloth folds, water splashes, fluid dynamics. The OpenAI
  Sora report framed video models as "world simulators" — a deliberately
  provocative claim. The truth is somewhere in between: the model has
  learned a *statistical* model of video physics that is *often* but not
  *always* consistent with reality.
- **Motion control**: text prompts are coarse for specifying motion.
  Image-to-video, video-to-video, and pose-controlled generation
  (DragNUWA, Motion Brush, Wan-controllable) emerged to give finer
  control.
- **Audio**: video without audio is not video. Veo 3 (May 2025) made the
  jointly-generated case mainstream; Movie Gen Audio also operates in
  this space.

<Callout variant="insight" title="The bitter lesson does video too">
Hand-engineered temporal modules (3D convs, optical-flow losses,
frame-interpolation networks) were the dominant approach pre-2023. They
all lost to "just feed spacetime patches into a big DiT." The same
pattern that played out in language and images played out in video on a
faster timescale because researchers knew what to try.
</Callout>

## The 2025–2026 frontier

- **Length**: Sora and Veo 3 produce minute-long videos at high resolution.
  Open weights (HunyuanVideo, Mochi) lag at 5–10 seconds but are catching up.
- **Interactivity**: world-model research (Genie 2, GameNGen, Oasis)
  treats video models as playable environments — generate the next frame
  *conditional on user inputs*. This blurs into RL territory.
- **Real-time**: distilled, few-step video models targeting streaming
  generation. Decart, Krea, and several research projects are pushing
  toward 30fps interactive generation.
- **3D and 4D**: per-pixel depth and Gaussian-splat reconstructions
  derived from generated video, enabling controllable camera trajectories
  and scene editing.

If you understand image DiTs and rectified flow, you already understand
85% of the video story. The remaining 15% is engineering: 3D VAEs,
sequence parallelism for long-context spacetime tokens, and the data
pipeline. Video generation is no longer a separate research community — it
is a special case of diffusion-Transformer engineering scaled to a third
dimension.
