For ten years the dream of general-purpose robotics was held back by a data
problem: robot data is expensive, robot data is embodiment-specific, and no
one could collect enough of it to train anything that generalized. By
2024-2026 a new pattern had emerged that finally cracked the cycle. **Take an
internet-pretrained vision-language model, fine-tune it on a giant pooled
dataset of real robot trajectories, and let it act.** That is a
vision-language-action (VLA) model.

It is the most exciting thing happening in robotics in a decade and worth
understanding even if you never touch a real robot.

## What a VLA actually is

A VLA model maps:

$$
(\text{image observation},\, \text{language instruction},\, \text{state}) \;\to\; \text{action}
$$

The image is from the robot's camera (or cameras). The instruction is natural
language ("put the apple in the bowl"). The state is the proprioceptive
reading (joint angles, gripper position). The action is whatever the robot
needs to execute next — typically end-effector pose deltas plus gripper
command, sometimes joint torques, sometimes a chunk of future actions.

The architecture is almost always:

- A **pre-trained VLM backbone** (PaLI, PaliGemma, LLaVA, Qwen-VL) for
  the perception + language part.
- An **action head** that converts the backbone's output into actions.

Two action-head designs dominate:

1. **Action tokenization** (RT-2, OpenVLA). Discretize each action dimension
   into ~256 bins. Reserve the highest-frequency token IDs in the
   vocabulary for action bins. Train autoregressively. Cheap; reuses the
   LLM head; loses precision.
2. **Continuous action chunks via flow matching** (π_0, π_0.5). Attach a
   small transformer that generates entire $H$-step continuous action
   sequences using flow matching (see part 6 topic 4). Faster at
   inference (single forward pass for $H$ steps), more expressive.

## The data flywheel

The single biggest unlock was *pooling data across robots*. The Open
X-Embodiment dataset (RT-X, 2023) collected ~1M trajectories from 22
embodiments at 21 institutions, covering everything from xArm-7 tabletop
manipulators to Stretch RE-1 mobile manipulators to bimanual ALOHA setups.

Pre-training on the mix gave RT-2-X measurable improvements on each
individual robot — even though no single robot saw all the data — because
the perception backbone benefited from variety and the action head learned
robust action representations across embodiments.

Physical Intelligence (founded 2024) pushed this further: π_0 was trained on
a curated mix of 7 single- and dual-arm embodiments and 68 task categories
covering everything from folding laundry to making an espresso. π_0.5 (2025)
added meaningful "open-vocabulary" generalization — the model performs new
tasks in homes it has never seen.

## Why language helps so much

Language is the wedge that makes pre-training transfer to robotics. Three
mechanisms are at play:

1. **Compositional task specification.** "Put the *blue* cup in the *left*
   drawer" mixes attributes the model has never seen together but knows
   individually.
2. **Visual grounding inherited from VLM pre-training.** A VLM that has
   seen 10B image-text pairs already has rich representations of "cup,"
   "drawer," "left." Robot fine-tuning teaches it how to *act* with those
   concepts; it does not have to learn what they look like.
3. **Chain-of-thought planning.** RT-2 and successors can be prompted to
   produce intermediate language reasoning (which object, then which
   action) before emitting actions, improving multi-step task success.

```python
# Conceptual VLA forward pass (OpenVLA style).
img    = robot_camera.read()                  # (H, W, 3)
prompt = f"In: What action should the robot take to {instruction}?\nOut:"
logits = vlm(image=img, text=prompt)          # next-token over vocab
action_tokens = logits.argmax(-1)[-7:]        # last 7 tokens = 7 action dims
action = detokenize(action_tokens)            # (Δx, Δy, Δz, Δrx, Δry, Δrz, gripper)
robot.execute(action)
```

## The honest frontier

VLAs work — convincingly, repeatably — on tabletop manipulation tasks within
the demonstrated distribution. They handle visual and linguistic variation
beyond what any prior approach could.

What they do *not* yet do:

- **Long-horizon tasks** (cook a meal, fold a basket of laundry end-to-end)
  remain beyond reliable single-policy execution. Hierarchical planning on
  top is an active research frontier.
- **Contact-rich manipulation** (peg insertion, deformable objects) is
  better with diffusion policies (see part 11 topic 8) than with VLAs.
  Hybrids are emerging.
- **Truly novel embodiments** (a humanoid trained on arm data) require
  more transfer than current methods support; this is what NVIDIA's GR00T
  (Jim Fan and team) is trying to solve at scale.

Three companies in mid-2026 are pushing the frontier hardest: Physical
Intelligence (π series), NVIDIA (GR00T), and Google DeepMind (RT-2 lineage,
Gemini Robotics). They each have different bets on the action head, the
backbone, and the embodiment scope.

<Callout variant="insight" title="The 'GPT-2 moment' framing">
Sergey Levine's framing — that VLAs in 2025-26 are the GPT-2 moment for
robotics — is probably the right calibration. They are real. They
generalize. They are far from a product. The next few years are about
whether the same scaling story (more data, bigger models, more diverse
tasks) will play out as it did for language. Bet on yes, with caveats.
</Callout>
