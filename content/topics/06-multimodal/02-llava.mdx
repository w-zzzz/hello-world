The LLaVA paper landed in April 2023 with a deceptively simple thesis: take a
strong vision encoder, take a strong LLM, glue them together with a small
projector, train on synthetic visual instruction data, and you get a
GPT-4V-quality model for a few thousand dollars. The recipe was so
unreasonably effective that nearly every open-weights vision-language model
since — Idefics, PaliGemma, MiniGPT-4, InternVL, Qwen-VL, Pixtral, Molmo —
is a variation on it.

If CLIP taught an image encoder to *speak the same language* as text, LLaVA
taught the LLM to *see*.

## The architecture, in three pieces

```
Image  →  [Vision encoder]  →  patch embeddings  →  [Projector]  →  visual tokens
                                                                          ↓
Text instruction  →  [Tokenizer]  →  text tokens  →  [LLM (causal)]  →  output text
                                                          ↑
                                                     visual tokens prepended
```

- **Vision encoder**: a frozen CLIP-ViT-L/14 in original LLaVA; SigLIP in
  most recent VLMs. It produces $n$ patch embeddings per image (e.g.,
  576 patches for 336×336 input).
- **Projector**: originally a single linear layer, later a 2-layer MLP. Its
  job is to map vision-encoder vectors into the LLM's input embedding
  space. Tiny in parameter count (~10M).
- **LLM**: any decoder-only model — Vicuna in original LLaVA, Llama 3 /
  Qwen / Gemma in newer ones.

At inference, the image becomes a sequence of visual tokens that the LLM
reads exactly like text. The whole interaction is autoregressive: the
model attends from text → image and from later text → earlier text and
images.

## Two-stage training

**Stage 1: Feature alignment.** Freeze both the vision encoder and the LLM.
Train *only* the projector on a corpus of (image, caption) pairs, with the
loss being next-token prediction of the caption given the visual tokens.
This is essentially teaching the projector to be a *translator* from
CLIP-space to LLM-token-space.

**Stage 2: Visual instruction tuning.** Unfreeze the LLM (the vision
encoder can stay frozen or be fine-tuned). Train on multimodal instruction
data — questions about images, conversational image analysis, visual
reasoning. LLaVA's clever trick was generating this data by feeding image
captions to GPT-4 (text-only at the time) and asking it to invent
plausible questions and answers. Synthetic instruction data, validated by
the eventual model's competence.

```python
from transformers import LlavaForConditionalGeneration, AutoProcessor
from PIL import Image

processor = AutoProcessor.from_pretrained("llava-hf/llava-1.5-7b-hf")
model = LlavaForConditionalGeneration.from_pretrained("llava-hf/llava-1.5-7b-hf")

img = Image.open("cat.jpg")
prompt = "USER: <image>\nWhat is in the image and what mood does it convey? ASSISTANT:"
inputs = processor(images=img, text=prompt, return_tensors="pt")
out = model.generate(**inputs, max_new_tokens=200)
print(processor.decode(out[0], skip_special_tokens=True))
```

That's a complete VLM inference call. The `<image>` placeholder is where
the projector's visual tokens are spliced in.

## Cross-attention vs prepending: two design philosophies

Flamingo (2022) did it differently: rather than prepend visual tokens, it
inserted *gated cross-attention* layers between the LLM's existing layers.
The visual information enters laterally; the original LLM's text-only
behavior is preserved when the gates are zero.

Trade-offs:

- **LLaVA-style prepending**: simpler, fewer new parameters, but the LLM's
  context length is consumed by visual tokens (576+ tokens per image is
  heavy on long-context tasks).
- **Flamingo-style cross-attention**: scales better to many images and
  videos (visual data doesn't bloat the context), but adds new layers and
  more complex training.

The community has mostly converged on LLaVA-style for single-image use
and Flamingo-style for video / many-image use.

<Callout variant="insight" title="Why so little new ML">
LLaVA contains essentially no new ML. CLIP existed. LLMs existed. Linear
projections existed. The contribution was *the recipe* — pick the right
components, freeze the right pieces, train on the right data, in the right
order. This is increasingly true of frontier work: the algorithms are old,
the engineering is new.
</Callout>

## Where VLMs are in 2026

- **Open-weights frontier**: Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, Pixtral, Molmo, Llama
  3.2-Vision — all within striking distance of GPT-4V on standard
  benchmarks.
- **Image resolution**: a major axis of progress. Early LLaVA was 336×336.
  Recent VLMs use dynamic tiling (slice large images into patches and feed
  each tile through the encoder) to handle 4K inputs.
- **Video**: extending LLaVA to video by sampling frames and feeding their
  visual tokens. LongVA, Video-LLaVA, and Qwen2.5-VL push toward
  hour-long video understanding.
- **Native multimodal**: GPT-4o and Gemini are trained from scratch with
  image, audio, and video tokens in the same model — bypassing the
  "encoder + LLM" decomposition entirely.

The frontier is shifting toward truly unified multimodal foundation models.
LLaVA's specific architecture may not survive that shift, but its lesson —
*language is a remarkably good interface for any modality* — almost
certainly will.
