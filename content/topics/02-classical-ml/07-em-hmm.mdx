The EM algorithm is the most important inference algorithm you have probably
never written from scratch. It is also the single conceptual ingredient that
made hidden Markov models tractable enough to power every speech recognition
system from 1980 to 2012, and it turns up in modern training recipes
disguised under different names.

This module covers EM, then HMMs as the canonical EM application, then a
brief look at where the ideas have ended up in 2026.

## Latent variables and the missing-data problem

You have data $x$ and a model with latent variables $z$:

$$
p(x \mid \theta) = \sum_z p(x, z \mid \theta)
$$

You want $\hat\theta_{\text{MLE}}$. Direct maximization is hard because the
sum-inside-log destroys the convenient structure of $\log p(x, z \mid \theta)$.
EM is the standard answer.

## The two steps

**E-step**: at the current $\theta^{(t)}$, compute the posterior over latents
$q(z) = p(z \mid x, \theta^{(t)})$ and form the *expected complete-data
log-likelihood*:

$$
Q(\theta \mid \theta^{(t)}) = \mathbb{E}_{q(z)}[\log p(x, z \mid \theta)]
$$

**M-step**: maximize $Q$ over $\theta$ to get $\theta^{(t+1)}$.

This guarantees $\log p(x \mid \theta^{(t+1)}) \ge \log p(x \mid \theta^{(t)})$ —
each iteration cannot decrease the marginal log-likelihood. (The proof is a
one-line application of Jensen's inequality.)

The view EM-as-coordinate-ascent on a free-energy functional generalizes to
**variational EM** when the exact posterior $p(z \mid x, \theta)$ is intractable;
you replace it with the closest tractable $q$. That is the bridge to VAEs,
which optimize an ELBO with the same shape.

## GMMs in three lines

For a $K$-component Gaussian mixture, the parameters are
$\theta = \\{\\pi_k, \mu_k, \Sigma_k\\}$.

- E-step: $\gamma_{ik} = \pi_k \mathcal{N}(x_i \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k) / \sum_j \pi_j \mathcal{N}(x_i \mid \mu_j, \Sigma_j)$
- M-step: $\pi_k \leftarrow \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i \gamma_{ik}$, weighted means and covariances for $\mu_k, \Sigma_k$.

Initialize with k-means, run a few dozen iterations, watch the components
peel off the data. This is how scikit-learn's `GaussianMixture` works under
the hood.

## Hidden Markov models

An HMM defines a discrete latent sequence $z_{1:T}$ evolving as a Markov chain
with transition matrix $A$, an emission distribution $p(x_t \mid z_t)$ (often
a Gaussian per state), and an initial distribution $\pi$.

Three inference questions, each with its own dynamic program:

| Question | Algorithm | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Likelihood $p(x_{1:T})$ | Forward | $O(TK^2)$ |
| Most probable path $\arg\max_{z} p(z \mid x)$ | Viterbi | $O(TK^2)$ |
| Marginal posteriors $p(z_t \mid x_{1:T})$ | Forward-Backward | $O(TK^2)$ |

The **Baum-Welch** algorithm is just EM applied to HMMs: forward-backward in
the E-step gives the expected sufficient statistics (state occupancy, state
transitions); the M-step is normalized counting.

```python
import numpy as np

def forward(pi, A, B):  # B[t, k] = p(x_t | z_t = k)
    T, K = B.shape
    alpha = np.zeros((T, K))
    alpha[0] = pi * B[0]
    for t in range(1, T):
        alpha[t] = (alpha[t-1] @ A) * B[t]
    return alpha  # alpha[t, k] = p(x_{1:t}, z_t = k)
```

This recursion was written in 1966. It still runs every time you tag a
part-of-speech sequence in NLTK, segment a CRISPR read, or align a chunk of
audio to a transcript.

## The thirty-year reign

For thirty years (~1980 to ~2012), every state-of-the-art speech recognizer
was a **GMM-HMM**: an HMM where each state's emission distribution was a
Gaussian mixture over MFCC features, trained with Baum-Welch and a
hand-engineered pronunciation lexicon. Why this won:

1. The conditional-independence assumptions (states Markov, observations
   conditionally independent given state) made $O(TK^2)$ inference possible.
2. Baum-Welch is convex enough on the M-step to be reliable.
3. Pronunciation models, language models, and acoustic models could be
   composed as weighted finite-state transducers, decoded jointly.

Then deep learning's acoustic features (DNN-HMM hybrids) and end-to-end
**CTC** (Graves 2006, deployed in DeepSpeech 2014) closed the gap. By 2017
attention-based models like *listen-attend-spell* and Transformer ASR took
over the leaderboard, and Whisper (2022) made GMM-HMMs feel like steam
engines.

But the *algorithmic* lineage is preserved: CTC marginalizes over latent
alignments by dynamic programming over a lattice — the spirit of forward
algorithm, now embedded in a deep net.

<Callout variant="insight" title="EM is hiding in modern training">
Wake-sleep training of Helmholtz machines, contrastive divergence in RBMs,
the E-step/M-step structure of variational autoencoders, even
expectation-style updates in some MoE routing — they are all EM in
different costumes. Once you can spot the pattern (alternate between
inferring latents and updating parameters that condition on those
inferred latents), you see it everywhere.
</Callout>
