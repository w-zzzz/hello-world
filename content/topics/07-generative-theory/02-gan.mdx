GANs taught the field that *direct distribution matching* is possible without
a likelihood. For about five years they were the dominant image generator.
Then diffusion ate their lunch. Understanding why both halves of that arc
happened is good training for thinking about future generative paradigms.

## Why this matters

The VAE assumes a tractable likelihood and pays for it with blurry samples.
GANs threw out the likelihood entirely. Instead of asking "what is the
probability of this image?", they ask "can a critic tell my samples apart
from the data?". When that question is answered correctly, the generator
*has* matched the data distribution — no density estimate required.

This is a fundamentally different idea: implicit density modeling via a
two-player game. It enabled the first photorealistic generative models
(Progressive GAN, BigGAN, StyleGAN) and inspired a generation of contrastive
and adversarial training tricks.

## The minimax game

Two networks. The **generator** $G$ maps noise $z \sim p(z)$ to samples
$G(z)$. The **discriminator** $D$ scores how likely an input came from the
data versus from $G$. The original objective:

$$
\min_G \max_D \; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\mathrm{data}}}[\log D(x)] + \mathbb{E}_{z \sim p(z)}[\log(1 - D(G(z)))]
$$

$D$ wants to assign high score to real, low score to fake. $G$ wants to fool
$D$. At the global optimum $p_G = p_{\mathrm{data}}$ and $D = 1/2$
everywhere — $D$ has nothing to discriminate.

In practice we replace $\log(1 - D(G(z)))$ with $-\log D(G(z))$ for $G$ (the
"non-saturating" loss), because the original term has near-zero gradient
when $D$ is winning, exactly when $G$ most needs a signal.

## Why training is hard

GAN training is a saddle-point problem, not a minimization. Two failure
modes dominate:

1. **Mode collapse.** $G$ finds a few high-quality samples that fool $D$ and
   stops exploring. The latent space collapses onto a tiny submanifold.

2. **Vanishing gradients.** When $D$ becomes too strong, its outputs
   saturate; $G$ receives no useful gradient. When $D$ is too weak, the game
   is uninformative.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def gan_step(real, G, D, opt_g, opt_d, z_dim, device):
    z = torch.randn(real.size(0), z_dim, device=device)
    fake = G(z)

    # discriminator
    d_real = D(real)
    d_fake = D(fake.detach())
    d_loss = F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(d_real, torch.ones_like(d_real)) \
           + F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(d_fake, torch.zeros_like(d_fake))
    opt_d.zero_grad(); d_loss.backward(); opt_d.step()

    # generator (non-saturating)
    d_fake = D(fake)
    g_loss = F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(d_fake, torch.ones_like(d_fake))
    opt_g.zero_grad(); g_loss.backward(); opt_g.step()
    return d_loss.item(), g_loss.item()
```

The Wasserstein GAN replaced JS divergence with the earth-mover distance,
which provides smooth gradients even when $p_G$ and $p_{\mathrm{data}}$ have
disjoint support. WGAN-GP enforces the required 1-Lipschitz critic via a
gradient penalty:

$$
\lambda \, \mathbb{E}_{\hat{x}} \big[ (\|\nabla_{\hat{x}} D(\hat{x})\|_2 - 1)^2 \big]
$$

## StyleGAN: control as architecture

NVIDIA's StyleGAN line (2018–2021) re-engineered the generator. Instead of
pumping noise through a stack of transposed convs, StyleGAN maps noise to a
**style vector** $w$, then injects $w$ into each layer via adaptive instance
normalization. Different layers control different scales of detail: coarse
layers determine pose and identity, mid layers determine features, fine
layers determine texture.

The result was the cleanest pre-diffusion image generation: the
"this-person-does-not-exist" era was StyleGAN. It also gave latent-space
*editing* a real foothold, since semantic axes (smile, age, pose) often line
up with linear directions in $w$-space.

<Callout variant="insight" title="Why diffusion replaced GANs">
GANs are fast at inference (one forward pass) and produce sharp samples,
but they are unstable to train, hard to scale to text conditioning, and
prone to mode dropping. Diffusion models inverted the trade-off: slower
inference, but stable likelihood-based training, well-defined log-likelihoods,
and clean conditioning via classifier-free guidance. By 2022 diffusion had
taken over text-to-image and never looked back.
</Callout>

## What you take away

GANs are still useful where speed matters (single-step inference, super
resolution, real-time avatar generation). The deeper lesson is methodological:
*you can fit a generative model without a tractable likelihood, by training
a second network to estimate divergences for you*. That idea reappears
everywhere — contrastive learning, energy-based models, adversarial
robustness, even some discriminator-based reward models in alignment.

If a paper has a critic in it, GANs are the prequel.
