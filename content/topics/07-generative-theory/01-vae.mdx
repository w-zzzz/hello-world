The VAE is the reason a lot of modern generative models *work*. Stable
Diffusion's latent space is a VAE. The probabilistic graphical-model framing
that VAEs popularized — encoder, decoder, latent prior, ELBO — is the lingua
franca of all latent-variable modeling.

If you understand the VAE, you understand half of what diffusion is doing.

## Why this matters

Pixels are the wrong space to model. They're high-dimensional, redundant,
and full of perceptually irrelevant variation (sensor noise, JPEG artifacts).
A good generative model should operate in a *compressed, semantic* space.

The VAE gives us two things at once: an **encoder** that compresses data into
a structured latent, and a **decoder** that maps latents back to data. Unlike
a vanilla autoencoder, the VAE imposes a probabilistic structure that makes
the latent space *generative* — sampling from the prior produces plausible
data, not nonsense.

<Callout variant="insight" title="The whole field's debt to VAEs">
Every text-to-image model you use ships with a VAE. Stable Diffusion encodes
images to a $4 \times 64 \times 64$ latent and runs diffusion there, then
decodes once at the end. The 8x compression is the difference between a
research toy and a product.
</Callout>

## The model

A VAE assumes data is generated by a two-step process:

1. Sample a latent $z \sim p(z) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)$.
2. Sample data $x \sim p_\theta(x|z)$ from a decoder.

The marginal $p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x|z) p(z)\,dz$ is what we'd like to
maximize. It's intractable. So we introduce an **encoder**
$q_\phi(z|x) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x), \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_\phi^2(x)))$
that approximates the posterior $p_\theta(z|x)$, and optimize a tractable
lower bound.

## The ELBO

By Jensen's inequality,

$$
\log p_\theta(x) \ge \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}[\log p_\theta(x|z)] - \mathrm{KL}(q_\phi(z|x) \Vert p(z))
$$

The right-hand side is the **evidence lower bound** (ELBO). Two terms:

- **Reconstruction**: how well the decoder reproduces $x$ from $z \sim q_\phi(z|x)$.
- **KL regularizer**: how close the posterior approximation is to the prior.

Maximizing the ELBO simultaneously trains a good autoencoder *and* keeps the
latent distribution close to a standard Gaussian — which means we can sample
new data by drawing $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and decoding.

## The reparameterization trick

The reconstruction term is an expectation over $q_\phi(z|x)$. To get a
gradient we need to backpropagate through the sampling step — but sampling
is not differentiable. The fix:

$$
z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x) \odot \epsilon, \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)
$$

Now the randomness lives in $\epsilon$, which has no learnable parameters,
and gradients flow cleanly through $\mu_\phi$ and $\sigma_\phi$. This single
trick is what made VAEs trainable end-to-end.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def vae_loss(x, encoder, decoder, beta=1.0):
    mu, log_var = encoder(x)                      # (B, d)
    eps = torch.randn_like(mu)
    z   = mu + torch.exp(0.5 * log_var) * eps     # reparameterized sample
    x_hat = decoder(z)
    recon = F.mse_loss(x_hat, x, reduction="sum")
    # KL(N(mu, sigma^2) || N(0, I)) closed-form:
    kl    = -0.5 * torch.sum(1 + log_var - mu.pow(2) - log_var.exp())
    return recon + beta * kl
```

## $\beta$-VAE and disentanglement

Setting $\beta > 1$ in the KL term encourages the encoder to use the latent
budget more parsimoniously. Empirically, this tends to align individual
latent dimensions with independent factors of variation in the data — pose,
lighting, identity. The result is *disentanglement*: latent traversal along
one axis changes one human-meaningful attribute.

This isn't free. Higher $\beta$ degrades reconstruction. Disentanglement
without supervision is also impossible in the worst case (Locatello et al.,
2019), so any disentanglement you observe relies on inductive biases that
are hard to characterize.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Posterior collapse">
A common failure: the decoder becomes powerful enough to generate $x$ without
using $z$, so the encoder's KL collapses to zero and $q_\phi(z|x) \approx p(z)$
for all $x$. The model degenerates into an unconditional generator. KL
annealing, free bits, and weaker decoders are the usual mitigations.
</Callout>

## Where VAEs sit today

Pure pixel-space VAEs are no longer state of the art for image generation.
GANs took over for sharpness; diffusion took over for quality and
controllability. But the VAE is still load-bearing — every modern latent
diffusion system uses one as the perceptual front-end. The framework also
underwrites variational inference broadly: VQ-VAE for tokenized images, NVAE
for hierarchical latents, and any place you see "ELBO" in a paper.

The ELBO and reparameterization trick are not historical curiosities. They
are the toolkit you'll reach for any time you need to learn a probabilistic
latent variable model end-to-end.
