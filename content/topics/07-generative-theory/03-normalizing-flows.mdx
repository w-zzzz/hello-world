Normalizing flows are the most mathematically elegant generative model: a
sequence of invertible neural transformations that turns a complicated data
distribution into a simple base distribution and back. No lower bound, no
adversary — just the change-of-variables formula, applied carefully.

## Why this matters

VAEs give you a lower bound on $\log p(x)$. GANs give you no likelihood at
all. For some problems — anomaly detection, scientific inference, model
comparison — you need the *real* number. Normalizing flows provide it.

The price is structural. Every layer must be invertible, and the log-Jacobian
of every layer must be computable. That constraint shapes everything about
how flows are designed.

## The change of variables, revisited

Let $f: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ be a smooth bijection and $z = f(x)$.
If $z$ has density $p_Z$, then $x$ has density

$$
p_X(x) = p_Z(f(x)) \, \big|\det J_f(x)\big|
$$

Taking logs,

$$
\log p_X(x) = \log p_Z(f(x)) + \log \big|\det J_f(x)\big|
$$

That's the entire framework. Choose $p_Z$ to be something simple
(a standard Gaussian). Build $f$ as a composition of invertible neural
layers. Train by maximum likelihood: minimize $-\log p_X(x)$ on data. Sample
by drawing $z$ and computing $f^{-1}(z)$.

The hard part is making $f$ invertible *and* having a tractable
$\log|\det J|$. A generic neural network has neither.

## Coupling layers: the workhorse trick

Real NVP introduced the **coupling layer**. Split the input
$x = (x_1, x_2)$ in half. Then define

$$
y_1 = x_1, \quad y_2 = x_2 \odot \exp(s(x_1)) + t(x_1)
$$

where $s$ and $t$ are arbitrary neural networks. Two beautiful properties:

1. **Invertible**: $x_2 = (y_2 - t(y_1)) \odot \exp(-s(y_1))$. The networks
   $s$ and $t$ don't even need to be invertible — they're only ever evaluated
   forward.
2. **Triangular Jacobian**: $\partial y_1 / \partial x_2 = 0$, so
   $|\det J| = \prod_i \exp(s_i(x_1))$. Compute the log-det in $O(d)$.

Stack many coupling layers, alternating which half gets transformed, and you
get an expressive bijection. Glow added invertible $1 \times 1$ convolutions
for richer mixing.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class CouplingLayer(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, dim, hidden):
        super().__init__()
        self.net = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(dim // 2, hidden), nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Linear(hidden, dim),       # outputs scale and shift
        )

    def forward(self, x):
        x1, x2 = x.chunk(2, dim=-1)
        s, t = self.net(x1).chunk(2, dim=-1)
        s = torch.tanh(s)                 # bound the scale for stability
        y2 = x2 * torch.exp(s) + t
        log_det = s.sum(dim=-1)
        return torch.cat([x1, y2], dim=-1), log_det

    def inverse(self, y):
        y1, y2 = y.chunk(2, dim=-1)
        s, t = self.net(y1).chunk(2, dim=-1)
        s = torch.tanh(s)
        x2 = (y2 - t) * torch.exp(-s)
        return torch.cat([y1, x2], dim=-1)
```

<Callout variant="info" title="Continuous flows: FFJORD and neural ODEs">
A different family models the flow as the solution of an ODE
$\frac{dx}{dt} = f_\theta(x, t)$. Then by the instantaneous change of
variables, $\partial \log p / \partial t = -\mathrm{tr}(\partial f / \partial x)$ —
no determinant, just a trace. This is the bridge to neural ODEs and, more
recently, to flow matching and rectified flow, which now power state-of-the-art
text-to-image generation.
</Callout>

## Where flows shine, and where they don't

Normalizing flows shine wherever you need an exact, differentiable
log-density. Likelihood-free inference (gravitational waves, particle
physics), variational posteriors with rich shapes (flow-based VAEs), and
density estimation in moderate dimensions all use them routinely.

They struggle on high-resolution images. The bijectivity constraint forces
the latent to have the same dimension as the data, every layer pays a
log-det tax, and depth is expensive. By 2022, diffusion models had displaced
flows as the go-to for pixel generation — though the *continuous* flow story
came back through flow matching, which trains a velocity field with a much
simpler objective and produces straighter, faster sampling trajectories.

Flows are the cleanest probabilistic story in generative modeling. Knowing
them gives you the right vocabulary for diffusion, flow matching, and any
future model where the change-of-variables formula does the heavy lifting.
