GPT is the architecture that broke the dam. Decoder-only Transformer plus
next-token prediction plus a lot of internet plus a lot of GPUs — that's the
recipe. Everything from Llama to Mistral to Gemini to Claude descends from
this lineage. Understanding GPT precisely is the price of admission to the
rest of modern AI.

The radical claim, in retrospect, was almost philosophical: if you ask a big
enough network to predict the next token on enough text, *understanding* will
emerge as a side effect of compression. The 2018 GPT-1 paper hedged. By
GPT-3 in 2020, the hedge was gone.

## Why decoder-only

Transformers come in three flavors: encoder-only (BERT), encoder-decoder (T5,
the original 2017 architecture), and decoder-only (GPT). For a long time it
was unclear which would dominate. The community converged on decoder-only
for one reason: **a single autoregressive objective is enough to do
everything**.

A decoder-only model can:

- Generate text (its native mode).
- Classify text — frame the label as the next token.
- Translate, summarize, answer questions — frame everything as text in, text out.
- Use tools, write code, follow instructions — same trick.

The encoder–decoder split made sense when the world looked like
"input language → output language." Once we accepted that *every* problem
could be cast as continuation, the decoder-only architecture became Pareto-optimal.

## The architecture, precisely

A GPT is a stack of $N$ identical Transformer blocks operating on a sequence
of $T$ token embeddings. Each block does, in pre-norm form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &\leftarrow x + \text{MHA}(\text{LN}(x)) \\
x &\leftarrow x + \text{MLP}(\text{LN}(x))
\end{aligned}
$$

The MLP is typically a two-layer feedforward with an inner dimension $4d$
(more recently, with SwiGLU it's $\sim 2.67 d$ at the same parameter count).
Self-attention uses a causal mask:

$$
A_{ij} = \begin{cases} \frac{q_i \cdot k_j}{\sqrt{d_k}} & j \le i \\ -\infty & j > i \end{cases}
$$

so token $i$ never sees the future. After the final block, a LayerNorm and a
linear projection (often weight-tied to the embedding matrix) produce logits
over the vocabulary.

## Training, in one equation

The loss is just cross-entropy on next-token prediction, averaged over a
massive corpus $\mathcal{D}$:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{D}} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p_\theta(x_t \mid x_{<t})
$$

Every training example contributes $T$ supervision signals, one per position,
all evaluated in parallel thanks to the causal mask. This density is part of
why scaling works: a 1T-token dataset gives $\sim$1T gradient signals, not
just one per document.

```python
import torch, torch.nn as nn, torch.nn.functional as F

class GPTBlock(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d, h):
        super().__init__()
        self.ln1 = nn.LayerNorm(d)
        self.ln2 = nn.LayerNorm(d)
        self.attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(d, h, batch_first=True)
        self.mlp = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(d, 4*d), nn.GELU(), nn.Linear(4*d, d))

    def forward(self, x, mask):
        h, _ = self.attn(self.ln1(x), self.ln1(x), self.ln1(x), attn_mask=mask)
        x = x + h
        x = x + self.mlp(self.ln2(x))
        return x

# causal mask: True where j > i (forbidden)
T = 1024
mask = torch.triu(torch.ones(T, T, dtype=torch.bool), diagonal=1)
```

This is essentially nanoGPT in 12 lines — the rest is tokenization, batching,
and an optimizer.

## Why GPT-2 → GPT-3 was a shock

GPT-2 (1.5B parameters, 2019) was a competent text generator. GPT-3 (175B,
2020) was something qualitatively different: you could *describe a task in
the prompt* and it would do it. Translation. Arithmetic. SQL. Analogies.
Nothing in the loss said "be a few-shot learner" — the capability emerged
because compressing the internet well *requires* the ability to follow
patterns.

The lesson, repeated since: don't assume the loss is the limit of what the
model is doing. A model trained to predict the next token has to model the
world that produces those tokens.

<Callout variant="insight" title="Compression as cognition">
A perfect next-token predictor on a corpus describing physics, mathematics,
and human reasoning would have to *implement* working theories of physics,
mathematics, and human reasoning internally. Next-token prediction is not a
toy objective — it is, plausibly, a complete one for any task expressible in
text.
</Callout>

## What changed from GPT-2 to GPT-4 (and Llama 3, Claude, Gemini)

The architecture barely changed. The recipe got more careful:

- **Pre-norm** instead of post-norm (training stability).
- **RoPE / ALiBi** instead of sinusoidal positional encodings (length generalization).
- **SwiGLU / GeGLU** instead of GELU MLPs (a small but consistent win).
- **Grouped-query attention (GQA / MQA)** to shrink KV-cache size at inference.
- **RMSNorm** instead of LayerNorm (slightly cheaper, equivalent in practice).
- **Mixture-of-Experts** in the largest models (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4).

But the *loss*, the *objective*, and the *core dataflow* are unchanged from
the 2018 paper. That's the punchline of the GPT story: a remarkably narrow
architectural recipe, scaled by orders of magnitude in compute and data,
covered most of the distance to general-purpose intelligence.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't confuse the model with the assistant">
"GPT-4" the *base* model and "ChatGPT" the *assistant* are not the same
thing. The base model is a next-token predictor on internet text. To turn it
into a useful chatbot you need supervised instruction tuning and RLHF on top
— covered in the next two topics. Confusing these is the source of half the
public confusion about LLMs.
</Callout>
