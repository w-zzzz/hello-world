Before GPT-3 ate the world, the dominant Transformer paradigm was BERT.
2018 to roughly 2021 was the encoder era: every NLP leaderboard was a fight
between BERT, RoBERTa, ALBERT, ELECTRA, DeBERTa. Then decoder-only LLMs
absorbed most of the public attention. But encoders never went away — they
just specialized.

If you do retrieval, classification, named-entity recognition, or any task
where you want a single dense vector per input, you are probably still using
a BERT descendant. Understanding *why* BERT and GPT diverged tells you
something deep about what self-supervision can and cannot extract.

## The MLM objective

BERT is trained to reconstruct masked tokens:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{MLM}} = -\mathbb{E}_{x, M} \sum_{i \in M} \log p_\theta(x_i \mid x_{\setminus M})
$$

where $M$ is a random subset of positions (typically 15%) replaced by a
special `[MASK]` token. The crucial difference from GPT: when predicting
$x_i$, the model can attend to *all* other positions, including those after
$i$. The attention is bidirectional.

This buys representational power. The contextual embedding of "bank" in *"I
sat on the river bank"* genuinely incorporates "river" — both forward and
backward influence. Decoder-only models can recover this through depth, but
encoders get it for free.

<Callout variant="info" title="Why exactly 15%?">
The 15% number is a lever: too low and you get little supervision per
example; too high and you destroy the context the model needs to reconstruct
the targets. The original BERT also reserves 10% of masked positions to
*not* be replaced and 10% to be replaced by a random token — small details
that subsequent models (RoBERTa, DeBERTa) tweaked or removed.
</Callout>

## Architecture: the same stack, used differently

A BERT block is structurally identical to a GPT block — multi-head
self-attention plus an MLP, with residuals and LayerNorm — minus the causal
mask. The model emits a contextual vector for every token; for downstream
tasks you typically:

- **Classify**: take the `[CLS]` token's final hidden state, feed to a linear classifier.
- **Tag** (NER, POS): apply a per-token head to every output position.
- **Embed**: mean-pool the token vectors and use the result as a sentence representation.

```python
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModel
import torch

tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("google-bert/bert-base-uncased")
model = AutoModel.from_pretrained("google-bert/bert-base-uncased")

inputs = tok("The river bank was muddy.", return_tensors="pt")
with torch.no_grad():
    out = model(**inputs).last_hidden_state  # (1, T, 768)
sentence_emb = out.mean(dim=1)               # mean-pool to (1, 768)
```

That sentence embedding lives in a 768-dim space where semantically similar
sentences are nearby. This is the foundation of vector search and most
modern RAG pipelines.

## What BERT can't do, and why decoder-only won

BERT cannot generate fluent text. The MLM objective trains $p(x_i \mid x_{\setminus i})$
in isolation — it does not factorize into a coherent sampling procedure for
producing arbitrary new sequences. People have tried: iterative refinement,
non-autoregressive generation, blockwise denoising. None of it matched the
quality of plain autoregressive decoding.

Decoder-only models, by contrast, *are* a sampling procedure. The same model
that scores a sequence can produce one. Once GPT-3 showed that scaling a
decoder gets you understanding *for free*, the asymmetry became fatal: why
maintain two architectures when one covers both?

## The 2024 encoder revival

In late 2024, Answer.AI and LightOn released **ModernBERT** — a 22-layer
encoder with a 8192-token context, RoPE, GeGLU, and Flash Attention. It
beat the original BERT by huge margins on retrieval, classification, and
code understanding while running faster on modern hardware. The lesson:
encoders had been frozen in 2019 while decoders absorbed every architectural
improvement. Bring those improvements back to encoders and they are still
the right tool for "produce a vector" tasks.

If your application is "given an input, produce a label, a score, or an
embedding" — not "produce more text" — you almost certainly want an encoder.
The decoder hype obscured this, but the production ML world quietly never
left.

## Practical takeaways

- For **classification, NER, retrieval, embeddings**: use BERT / RoBERTa / DeBERTa / ModernBERT.
- For **generation, chat, agents, code**: use decoder-only.
- For **embedding-quality leaderboards** (MTEB), encoder backbones still dominate, often paired with contrastive fine-tuning.

The encoder era ended as the dominant paradigm, not as a useful one.
