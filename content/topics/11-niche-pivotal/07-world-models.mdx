A world model is a learned predictor of environment dynamics. Given the
current state and an action, it forecasts the next state. In its simplest
form it's just a self-supervised next-state model — but the consequences
are large: anything you can predict, you can also *plan* over, *imagine*
counterfactuals about, and *train policies* against without touching
the real world.

Three flavors are visibly defining the 2024–2026 frontier: **DreamerV3**
(latent dynamics for RL), **V-JEPA / V-JEPA 2** (representation prediction
from passive video, LeCun's program), and **Genie 2/3** (foundation
world models that produce playable environments from a prompt). All
three share a thesis: you don't need to predict pixels to capture the
world's structure.

## The agent-side picture: Dreamer

Hafner's Dreamer line (2019, V2 in 2020, V3 in 2023) is the canonical
RL world model. Architecture:

- An **encoder** $z_t = e(x_t)$ maps observations to a compact latent.
- A **recurrent dynamics** model $z_{t+1} = f(z_t, a_t)$ predicts the
  next latent given an action.
- A **decoder** reconstructs observations (only used during training).
- A **reward predictor** $\hat r_t = g(z_t)$.
- An actor-critic operates *entirely in latent space* — it never
  touches pixels at training time.

Training is two-phase. First, learn the dynamics from real environment
data (a few million frames). Then *imagine* trajectories by rolling
$f$ forward and train the actor-critic on those imagined rollouts. Real
environment data is the expensive resource; imagination is cheap.

DreamerV3's headline result: a single fixed hyperparameter setting reaches
expert-level performance on 150+ tasks across Atari, Crafter, Minecraft,
and continuous control. World-model RL had finally crossed the threshold
where one recipe handles everything — like the GPT moment for RL.

$$
\hat z_{1:H} \sim f(z_0, \pi_\theta), \qquad J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{H} \gamma^t \hat r_t\right]
$$

The actor maximizes expected reward in *imagined* trajectories, where the
horizon $H$ can be set to 15–20 latent steps for cheap planning.

## V-JEPA: predict representations, not pixels

LeCun's long-running argument is that pixel prediction is the wrong
objective for self-supervised learning. Most pixel variation is
unpredictable noise — exact texture, lighting, motion blur — and
optimizing MSE on it wastes capacity. Better: predict the *features* of a
masked region from the *features* of the visible region.

That's what V-JEPA does for video. Given a video clip, mask out a
spatiotemporal region. Encode visible and masked regions separately. Train
a predictor to map the visible features to the masked-region features
under an L2 loss in latent space:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{V-JEPA}} = \big\| s_\psi(z_{\text{masked}}) - z_{\text{target}} \big\|_2^2
$$

with a stop-gradient on the target (otherwise the trivial collapse to
constant features wins). The result: a video encoder that learns
high-quality features for downstream tasks (action recognition, motion
classification) without ever generating pixels.

V-JEPA 2 (Meta, late 2024) scales this up and demonstrates planning over
the learned latent dynamics — bridging the gap between "good
representations" and "useful for control."

## Genie: foundation world models

DeepMind's Genie (2024) and Genie 2/3 (2024–2025) take a different cut:
train a single model on enormous amounts of *unlabeled* video (gameplay,
robotic manipulation, driving) and learn a controllable simulator. From
a single prompt image, Genie produces a playable environment — the user's
keystrokes map to a learned discrete *latent action vocabulary*, and the
model rolls the video forward conditioned on those actions.

Three components:

- A **video tokenizer** that compresses frames to discrete tokens.
- A **latent action model** that infers per-step actions from
  consecutive frames in unlabeled video.
- A **dynamics model** that predicts next-frame tokens conditioned on
  prior frames and the latent action.

At inference, replace the inferred action with a user-controlled one.
Genie 2 produces minute-long, navigable 3D environments. Genie 3
extended this further with consistency and longer rollouts.

The implication is large: **world models can be pre-trained at LLM scale
on passive video** and used downstream for both simulation and as
synthetic-data engines for embodied agents.

```python
# Sketch: training a Dreamer-style latent dynamics model
import torch.nn.functional as F

def world_model_loss(encoder, dynamics, decoder, x, a):
    # x: (B, T, C, H, W) frames; a: (B, T, A) actions
    z = encoder(x)                                  # (B, T, D)
    z_pred = dynamics(z[:, :-1], a[:, :-1])         # (B, T-1, D)
    x_recon = decoder(z)
    recon_loss = F.mse_loss(x_recon, x)
    dyn_loss  = F.mse_loss(z_pred, z[:, 1:].detach())
    return recon_loss + dyn_loss
```

## Why world models matter now

Three reasons world models moved from niche to load-bearing in 2024–2026:

1. **Sample efficiency for embodied AI.** Robots can't easily collect
   millions of episodes of every behavior. A learned world model lets
   you train policies in imagination.
2. **Counterfactual reasoning for safety.** GAIA-1 and similar driving
   models let autonomy stacks ask "what if I turn left now?" without
   real-world risk.
3. **Synthetic data for foundation models.** A scaled video world model
   produces rollouts that are training data for *other* models —
   particularly robotic policies and embodied agents.

## Open questions

- **Long-horizon prediction.** Latents drift over long rollouts. How to
  ground them periodically (against real frames? language anchors?) is
  open.
- **Action vocabularies.** Genie's latent actions are discrete and
  inferred. The right action representation for arbitrary domains is
  unsettled.
- **Bridging language and dynamics.** Most LLMs have weak embodied
  intuition. Most world models have weak language. Hybrid architectures
  (vision-language-world models) are the live research frontier.

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't confuse video generation with world modeling">
Sora makes plausible-looking video. That's not the same as a *useful
world model* — a world model has to be controllable, consistent, and
support counterfactuals. Sora's outputs often violate physics in ways
that would catastrophically misguide a planner. The two research programs
are converging but are not the same thing yet.
</Callout>
