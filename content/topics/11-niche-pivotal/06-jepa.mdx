Most self-supervised learning trained on images either contrastively
(CLIP, SimCLR — pull positive pairs together, push everything else
apart) or generatively (MAE — reconstruct masked pixels). **JEPA** —
the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture, championed by Yann LeCun
and Meta — proposes a third path: predict the *embeddings* of masked
regions, not the pixels. The image, video, and audio variants of this
idea are some of the most promising non-contrastive SSL methods in
2024–2026.

## Why this matters

LeCun's argument is that current generative SSL wastes capacity on
modeling unpredictable, semantically irrelevant detail (the exact
texture of grass, the precise shape of a cloud). A model that has to
generate *pixels* must commit predictions to every variation. A model
that predicts *features* gets to ignore what doesn't matter and focus
on what does.

This isn't only philosophical. I-JEPA matches or beats MAE on most
linear-probe benchmarks at significantly less compute, and V-JEPA
extends the recipe to video — the natural setting for "world model"
ambitions.

## The architecture

JEPA has three modules:

- **Context encoder** $E_\theta$ that processes the visible patches.
- **Target encoder** $E_\xi$ that processes the *full* image; weights
  are an exponential moving average of $E_\theta$ (no gradient flow).
- **Predictor** $P_\phi$ that takes the context's output plus a
  positional spec for the masked region and predicts what the target
  encoder produces there.

The training signal is a regression loss in feature space:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{m \in \text{masks}} \big\| P_\phi(E_\theta(x_\text{ctx}), m) - \mathrm{sg}\big[E_\xi(x_\text{full})\big]_m \big\|^2
$$

`sg` is stop-gradient. The target side does not learn directly from
loss; only from EMA of the context side's improvements. This
asymmetric structure is critical.

## Why it doesn't collapse

Predicting features into another network's features sounds like a
recipe for **representation collapse**: both sides converging to a
constant. If you let gradients flow through both encoders, that's
exactly what happens. The fix, inherited from BYOL, is the
**stop-gradient + EMA** asymmetry. The target encoder is a slowly
moving teacher; the context encoder cannot influence it directly. Empirically
and (after several years of analysis) somewhat theoretically, this is
enough to prevent collapse.

<Callout variant="insight" title="The non-contrastive SSL puzzle">
For years it was thought that you needed contrastive negatives to
prevent collapse. BYOL showed this was false; JEPA inherits the trick.
We now have multiple methods that learn rich features without
explicit negative samples — the practical implication is *much*
smaller batch sizes and simpler infrastructure.
</Callout>

## I-JEPA in practice

The image version (I-JEPA) takes a Vision Transformer's patches as
context, masks several large blocks, and predicts the target
encoder's embeddings in the masked blocks from the context encoder's
embeddings of the visible blocks.

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def ijepa_step(context_enc, target_enc, predictor, x):
    # x: (B, C, H, W), patchified into N tokens
    ctx_idx, tgt_idx = sample_block_masks(num_patches=N)
    ctx_tokens = context_enc(x, idx=ctx_idx)              # context features
    with torch.no_grad():
        tgt_tokens = target_enc(x)[..., tgt_idx, :]       # target features
    pred = predictor(ctx_tokens, mask_positions=tgt_idx)
    loss = F.smooth_l1_loss(pred, tgt_tokens)
    return loss
```

The target encoder's parameters are updated as an EMA of the context
encoder's:

$$
\xi \leftarrow \tau \xi + (1 - \tau) \theta
$$

with $\tau$ close to 1 (e.g., 0.996, ramped to 1 over training).

## V-JEPA: the video version

V-JEPA (2024) extends this to video. Now the masked regions can span
both space and time, and the model must predict features for unseen
spatiotemporal blocks. The information available in observed frames
constrains motion, object identity, and (sometimes) causal structure.

The argument is that V-JEPA learns useful **temporal predictability**
without ever generating a frame. A 16-frame clip with half its patches
masked across space and time gives a strong learning signal in feature
space; the same setup at the pixel level would force the model to
predict precise textures it cannot know.

Empirically, V-JEPA features transfer well to action recognition and
video understanding, often beating models that were trained
generatively at the pixel level.

## The world-models bet

LeCun's larger argument — laid out in *A Path Towards Autonomous
Machine Intelligence* — is that LLMs alone cannot reach human-level
intelligence because they lack a *world model*: a learned, predictive
internal simulator. Pixel-generative models nominally have such a
simulator, but at the wrong level of abstraction. JEPA-style predictive
models, in this view, learn the right kind of simulator because they
predict in the space of meaningful features.

Whether this is correct is a live debate. The position is internally
consistent and produces strong empirical artifacts (I-JEPA, V-JEPA,
audio JEPA). The counterposition — that scaling generative training
keeps producing capability gains, including emergent world-model-like
behavior — is also empirically backed.

<Callout variant="warning" title="The honest caveat">
JEPA performs strongly on benchmarks but does not yet have a clear
'world-model emergent behavior' demonstration analogous to ICL in
language models. The bet is theoretically motivated and empirically
promising; the verdict is open.
</Callout>

## What to read next

- LeCun's "Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" position
  paper for the philosophical frame.
- The I-JEPA and V-JEPA papers for the methods.
- BYOL and DINO/DINOv2 for adjacent non-contrastive SSL — the same
  asymmetry trick under different objectives.
- Bardes et al.'s VICReg work for an information-theoretic alternative
  to BYOL/JEPA's collapse-avoidance.

If you internalize "predict in feature space, not pixel space," much of
the current SSL frontier becomes legible — and the ongoing JEPA-vs-
generative-SSL debate becomes a genuine question rather than a slogan.
